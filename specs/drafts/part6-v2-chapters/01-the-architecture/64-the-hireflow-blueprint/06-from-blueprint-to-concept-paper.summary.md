### Core Concept

The Factory Blueprint is not the final artifact; it is the starting artifact. The blueprint sits at the end of Phase 1 (Explore) in the Agent Maturity Model and feeds directly into the 10-80-10 Concept Paper in Chapter 65, which then unlocks Phase 2 (Incubate).

### Key Mental Models

- **Blueprint vs. Concept Paper**: The blueprint is an architecture document (what the factory does). The concept paper is a persuasion document (why the factory should exist). The blueprint's domain knowledge forms the 80% of the 10-80-10 rule.
- **Blueprint as seed**: Role specifications feed Chapter 67 (agent skills). Data contracts feed Chapter 76 (database schemas). Human review gates feed Chapter 82 (API endpoints). Economic participation points feed Chapter 84 (orchestrator). The blueprint is not consumed and discarded; it is referenced throughout Part 6.
- **Systems thinking as an active skill**: James finds three gaps by reading his own blueprint: missing ambiguity handling in the Job Spec Writer, insufficient data passed to the Summarizer from the Screener, and an undefined "confidence" term in Gate 1's trigger. This is the lesson's core pedagogical point.

### Critical Patterns

- The chapter-to-chapter chain is short and concrete: Blueprint (Ch 64) feeds Concept Paper (Ch 65) feeds Agent Skills (Ch 67)
- Blueprint is complete at the end of Phase 1; concept paper closes Phase 1; Phase 2 begins with skill extraction
- Every gap James finds has a structural type: missing specification field, incomplete data contract, undefined term in a trigger condition

### Common Mistakes

- Treating the blueprint as a one-time document rather than a reference that grows as gaps are found
- Confusing the blueprint with the concept paper (they have different purposes and different audiences)
- Not reading the blueprint critically after writing it; Emma sets a time-boxed review specifically because gaps surface on re-reading
