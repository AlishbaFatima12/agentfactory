# Three Frames for the Factory

James and Emma stood at the whiteboard. The diagrams from the past five lessons filled every surface: the four chatbot boxes (crossed out), the Two-Layered Model, the governance loop, the HireFlow pipeline.

"We covered a lot," James said. "But I feel like there's more."

"There is. What you learned today is one of three lenses for understanding agent factories. Each lens answers a different question."

## Frame 1: The Two-Layered Model (Governance)

**Question it answers:** Who governs the factory, and how?

This is what you learned in this chapter. The Factory Layer produces outcomes under organizational governance. The Edge Layer amplifies individual humans. Intent flows down from humans, verification flows up from the factory, and outcomes flow back for review.

The Two-Layered Model tells you where the boundaries are. What the organization controls. What the individual controls. How the two layers interact without stepping on each other.

"This is the 'who's in charge' frame," James said.

"Precisely. And it matters because without governance, the factory drifts."

## Frame 2: The Agent Maturity Model (Journey)

**Question it answers:** How does a factory grow from idea to production?

Factories are not built in a single leap. There is a journey from "I have an idea for an AI hiring system" to "HireFlow screens 500 candidates a day at 95% accuracy," and that journey has phases. Chapter 63 maps those phases and introduces the concept of **crystallization**: the moment when general experimentation produces enough clarity to build a specialist. James's chatbot army in Lesson 2 was an attempt to skip straight to the end; the Maturity Model shows why that fails and what comes first.

## Frame 3: Agents as Economic Actors (Future)

**Question it answers:** What happens when agents can acquire their own resources?

Today, agents consume resources that humans allocate: API credits, compute, storage. Chapter 62 explores what changes when agents become **economic actors** that evaluate costs and acquire resources within budget constraints. You will not build an agent that buys things in Part 6, but you will build the plumbing (budget tracking, audit trails, spending envelopes) that economic participation rides on. Retrofitting that plumbing later is a rewrite; designing for it now is a few extra tables.

:::note Which frame would help?
Suppose a hiring manager asks: "Should I build one general-purpose recruitment agent or four specialists?" Which of the three frames helps you answer that question? (Hint: it is the one concerned with phases and readiness, not governance or economics.)
:::

## How the Three Frames Connect

Each frame is a lens on the same factory. They are not alternatives; they are complementary perspectives.

| Frame                    | Question             | Chapter              | What It Gives You                                        |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Two-Layered Model**    | Who governs?         | Ch 61 (this chapter) | Governance architecture: layers, contracts, verification |
| **Agent Maturity Model** | How does it grow?    | Ch 63                | Development roadmap: phases, gates, crystallization      |
| **Economic Actors**      | What is it becoming? | Ch 62                | Future-ready design: budgets, audits, resource tracking  |

"You need all three to build a real factory," Emma said. "The Two-Layered Model tells you the structure. The Maturity Model tells you the sequence. The Economic Actors frame tells you what to design for that you do not need today but will need tomorrow."

James looked at the whiteboard one more time. Four chatbot boxes, crossed out. A pipeline with contracts and verification. Two layers. A governance loop. Three frames.

"I didn't build anything today," he said. "But I understand what I'm about to build. And why the chatbot approach would have failed."

"That understanding is the foundation. Everything in Chapters 62 through 90 sits on top of it."
