### Core Concept

The chapter quiz consolidates understanding of the five foundational concepts from Chapter 61: the chatbot-army failure modes, the Two-Layered Model, the governance loop, Digital FTEs, and the three frames. Ten multiple-choice questions test whether the reader can apply these concepts to scenarios, not just recall definitions.

### Key Mental Models

- **Application over recall**: The quiz tests scenario-based reasoning: "Why is 'Find me good candidates' bad intent?" targets the verifiability requirement, not the memorization of the word "governance."
- **Self-assessment framework**: Score 9-10 = proceed; 7-8 = review referenced lessons; 5-6 = re-read L2-L4; below 5 = full chapter review. The quiz doubles as a diagnostic.
- **Reflection prompts**: Three open-ended questions target the deepest understanding checks: explain the Two-Layered Model in two sentences, draw the HireFlow pipeline from memory, and identify the hardest concept.

### Critical Patterns

- Question 5 (chatbot silent failure) is the most representative diagnostic question: getting it wrong signals the reader understood the surface narrative but missed the structural lesson about contracts.
- Question 9 (why both layers are required) tests the most counterintuitive insight: a fully governed Factory Layer still fails without the Edge Layer because humans need a conversational interface to the factory's structured output.
- The chapter reflection on governance being abstract "until you see it fail" is a forward pointer: Chapter 68 (Simulation-Driven Validation) is designed to make governance concrete through a simulated failure scenario.

### Common Mistakes

- Answering Question 1 with "more powerful language models": this is the most common wrong instinct and signals that the reader equated AI capability with factory architecture.
- Confusing "verification at every handoff" with "one gate at the end" (Question 6): this distinction is the most practically consequential concept in Chapter 61.
