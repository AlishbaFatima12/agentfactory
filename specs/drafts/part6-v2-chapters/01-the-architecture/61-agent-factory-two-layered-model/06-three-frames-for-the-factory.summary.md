### Core Concept

Three complementary conceptual frames govern all of Part 6. Each answers a different question about agent factories, and all three are necessary for complete understanding. The Two-Layered Model answers "who governs?" The Agent Maturity Model answers "how does the factory grow?" Agents as Economic Actors answers "what is it becoming?" No single frame is sufficient; together they produce a complete architectural lens.

### Key Mental Models

- **Frame 1: Two-Layered Model (governance)**: Defines the boundaries between organizational control and individual control, how they interact without stepping on each other, and why the factory drifts without governance.
- **Frame 2: Agent Maturity Model (journey)**: Maps the five-phase path from general experimentation to specialized production. Crystallization is the moment when exploration produces enough clarity to build a specialist. James's chatbot-army impulse was an attempt to skip straight to the end of this journey.
- **Frame 3: Economic Actors (future)**: Addresses what happens when agents evaluate costs and acquire resources within budget constraints. You will not build an agent that buys things in Part 6, but you will build the audit trails and budget envelopes that economic participation requires. Retrofitting is a rewrite; designing for it now is a few extra tables.

### Critical Patterns

- The three frames are not alternatives: they are complementary lenses on the same factory. A factory designer needs all three.
- The maturity model frame is the answer to "should I build one general agent or four specialists?": the question is about readiness and phase, not governance or economics.
- By the end of this lesson, James has learned no code but has built the mental foundation that every chapter from 62 to 90 builds on. Understanding why precedes understanding how.

### Common Mistakes

- Conflating the three frames or treating one as primary: governance, journey, and economic design are each required; none subsumes the others.
- Skipping the maturity model because "I know what I want to build": the maturity model exists precisely for builders who think they know the destination and try to build the specialist before building the explorer.
