### Core Concept

The three conceptual frames introduced across Chapters 61-63 are not alternatives; they are complementary lenses on the same factory. The Two-Layered Model answers "who governs." The Economic Actors frame answers "what is it becoming." The Agent Maturity Model answers "how does it grow." Together, they provide the complete architectural foundation before any code is written.

### Key Mental Models

- **The Maturity Model as dependency chain**: not a bureaucratic checklist but a sequence in which each phase produces inputs the next phase requires
- **Architecture preparation vs. Phase 1**: Chapters 61-64 give developers the three frames and the factory blueprint; this preparation focuses Phase 1 exploration so it is not aimless
- **The Three Frames synthesis**:
  - Two-Layered Model: who runs the factory (structure)
  - Economic Actors: what the factory will become (design for)
  - Maturity Model: the sequence from idea to production (how to get there)

### Critical Patterns

- The Architecture section (Chapters 61-64) is preparation, not Phase 1; Phase 1 begins in Chapter 65
- James's supply chain metaphor holds: bad raw materials (skipped Phases 1-2) produce bad finished goods (defective specialists) regardless of how well the assembly line (Phase 3) runs
- The three frames are designed to be used in parallel: blueprinting HireFlow in Chapter 64 draws on all three simultaneously

### Common Mistakes

- Treating the Maturity Model as optional overhead rather than a dependency chain
- Conflating "architecture preparation" with "Phase 1 has begun"; the chapter structure is deliberate
