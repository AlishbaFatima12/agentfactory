### Core Concept

A general agent plays two distinct roles in factory development. As Incubator (Director), it explores the domain alongside the builder: drafting concept papers, challenging assumptions, generating test scenarios. As Builder, it constructs specialist agents from validated knowledge. These two roles are separated by crystallization: the phase transition when domain exploration becomes structured enough to build from.

### Key Mental Models

- **Incubator (Director role)**: the general agent as research partner, exploring domain requirements before any specialist is constructed
- **Specialist**: a purpose-built, narrow agent with validated skills, domain-specific rubrics, and tested edge case handling, not a general agent's guess
- **Crystallization**: the moment exploration solidifies into specifications; in Part 6, triggered by the 9.5+ quality threshold
- **The key insight**: general agents BUILD custom agents. They do not become them. The construction crew and the workforce are different entities.

### Critical Patterns

- The general agent is too broad, expensive, and unpredictable for production specialist work; a narrow, reliable agent is purpose-built instead
- Crystallization is both a decision (to evaluate) and a measurement (the threshold confirms readiness); neither alone suffices
- The 9.5+ threshold protects against the most common failure: starting to build because "it feels ready enough"

### Common Mistakes

- Trying to teleport from IDEA directly to PRODUCTION, skipping the Incubator work entirely (James's mistake in Lesson 1)
- Believing the general agent will transform into the specialist through prompting alone
- Treating crystallization as a subjective judgment call rather than a measured threshold
