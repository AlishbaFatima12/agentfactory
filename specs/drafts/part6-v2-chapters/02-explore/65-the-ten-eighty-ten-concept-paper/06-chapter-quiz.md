# Chapter Quiz

Test your understanding of the 10-80-10 Concept Paper. Answer all 10 questions. Aim for 80% or higher.

---

**Question 1.** What is the primary difference between a Factory Blueprint and a Concept Paper?

A. The blueprint is longer and more detailed than the concept paper.
B. The blueprint describes what a factory does; the concept paper argues that it should exist.
C. The concept paper replaces the blueprint once validation is complete.
D. The blueprint is written by AI; the concept paper is written by humans.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The blueprint is an architecture document (what the factory does, how parts connect). The concept paper is a validation document (should this factory exist in this form, are the assumptions sound). Both are written by the reader. The concept paper does not replace the blueprint; it validates it.

</details>

---

**Question 2.** In the 10-80-10 Rule, the eighty percent segment represents:

A. AI-generated analysis of the domain
B. Literature review and academic research
C. The reader's domain knowledge, primarily from the Factory Blueprint
D. Feedback from the multi-LLM advisory board

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The 80% is the reader's own domain knowledge, structured formally in the Factory Blueprint (Chapter 64). This is the core substance of the concept paper. It is NOT AI-generated content.

</details>

---

**Question 3.** Why does the 10-80-10 Rule place AI validation at the end rather than the beginning?

A. AI models are not available during the research phase.
B. The reader must first develop enough domain understanding to evaluate AI feedback.
C. AI validation is optional and only needed if the reader is uncertain.
D. AI models perform better when given a complete document rather than individual claims.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** Without the domain understanding built through research (10%) and blueprint work (80%), the reader cannot evaluate whether AI feedback is correct, relevant, or applicable to their specific factory. The AI validation sharpens what the reader wrote; it does not substitute for the reader's judgment.

</details>

---

**Question 4.** A reader finds they need 30% of their concept paper time on research because their blueprint has many unverified assumptions. What does this indicate?

A. The 10-80-10 Rule should be adjusted to 30-50-20 for this domain.
B. The reader should use AI to fill the research gaps faster.
C. The reader is not ready for a concept paper and should return to domain exploration.
D. The research phase should be skipped to save time.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The 10-80-10 ratio is also a diagnostic. If the reader needs significantly more than 10% on research, their domain knowledge (the 80%) is insufficient. They should return to Phase 1 exploration and strengthen their understanding before writing the concept paper.

</details>

**Question 5.** In the Advisory Board Pattern, three LLMs are assigned the roles of Domain Challenger, Logic Auditor, and Gap Finder. Why use three different models rather than asking one model three times with different prompts?

A. Different models are trained on different data, so they have different blind spots.
B. Using three models is always faster than three sequential prompts to one model.
C. One model cannot be given different prompts in the same session.
D. The three roles require fundamentally different AI architectures.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A.** Different frontier models have different training data, reasoning patterns, and strengths. One model might excel at finding logical inconsistencies while another is stronger on domain knowledge. Asking one model three times produces three variations of the same model's blind spots.

</details>

**Question 6.** James receives feedback from the Logic Auditor: "Section 2 claims that human review gates preserve judgment, but Section 4 argues each FTE should fail independently. These claims appear to contradict each other." How should James classify this feedback?

A. Stylistic suggestion: ignore it.
B. Substantive critique: revise the paper to resolve the contradiction.
C. Missing section: add a new section about gate-FTE interaction.
D. Research contradiction: investigate external sources.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** This is a substantive critique: an internal logical contradiction between two sections of the paper. James should revise by clarifying the relationship (e.g., FTEs are independently deployable, but pipeline-level gates coordinate their interaction).

</details>

**Question 7.** What is the crystallization signal?

A. The moment when all three advisory board models give the same rating.
B. The shift in AI feedback from substantive critique to stylistic suggestion.
C. The point at which the concept paper reaches exactly 10 pages.
D. When the reader stops finding new information during the research phase.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The crystallization signal is when AI advisors stop finding substance to challenge (missing knowledge, logical contradictions, unstated assumptions) and start offering polish (reorder sections, add mentions, improve phrasing). This shift indicates the paper has matured past the point of structural weakness.

</details>

**Question 8.** A concept paper receives these scores after the third advisory board loop: Domain Challenger 9.6, Logic Auditor 9.7, Gap Finder 9.5. The feedback includes: "Consider mentioning seasonal hiring volumes" and "The argument is coherent." Should the reader continue iterating?

A. Yes, because the average (9.6) has not reached 10.0.
B. Yes, because the Gap Finder's score is exactly 9.5, which is the minimum threshold.
C. No, because the scores are above 9.5 AND the feedback has shifted to stylistic suggestions.
D. No, because three loops is always sufficient regardless of scores.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C.** The 9.5+ threshold is met AND the feedback is stylistic ("consider mentioning," "the argument is coherent"). Both conditions are satisfied. Continuing to iterate past crystallization is "procrastination disguised as quality."

</details>

**Question 9.** In the concept paper template, the FTE Justification section tests each FTE against three criteria: Distinct Input, Distinct Expertise, and Independent Failure. If the Interview Question Generator receives input from BOTH the Resume Screener and the Job Spec Writer, does it fail the Distinct Input criterion?

A. Yes, because it shares an input source with the Resume Screener.
B. No, because the synthesis of two input streams IS the distinct expertise.
C. Yes, because all FTEs must receive input from exactly one source.
D. No, because the criterion only checks whether the input is different from adjacent FTEs, not how many sources there are.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The Interview Question Generator's distinct value is the synthesis of screening results with job specifications. Neither upstream FTE performs this combination. The criterion asks whether the FTE receives data "from a different source than adjacent FTEs," and the combined input qualifies as distinct because no other FTE uses that specific combination.

</details>

**Question 10.** The concept paper sits at the boundary between which two phases of the Agent Maturity Model?

A. Phase 2 (Incubate) and Phase 3 (Build Specialist)
B. Phase 1 (Explore) and Phase 2 (Incubate)
C. Phase 3 (Build Specialist) and Phase 4 (Integrate)
D. The concept paper is not part of the Agent Maturity Model.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B.** The concept paper closes Phase 1 (Explore). After the Domain Mastery Gate (Chapter 66), Phase 2 (Incubate) begins with skill extraction (Chapter 67). The concept paper is the last thinking artifact before prototyping begins.

</details>
