### Core Concept

The 10-80-10 Rule is the structure for writing a concept paper that earns the right to proceed to implementation. Ten percent is targeted research on specific assumption gaps. Eighty percent is domain knowledge, primarily from the Factory Blueprint. Ten percent is AI validation: using a frontier LLM to attack the paper, not write it.

### Key Mental Models

- **Research as Gap Closure, Not Literature Review**: The first 10% targets 3-5 specific assumptions the builder cannot defend with evidence. It is not "learn the domain from scratch." If research needs more than 10%, the builder does not know the domain well enough for a concept paper.
- **The Blueprint IS the 80%**: The Factory Blueprint (Ch 64) already contains the domain knowledge. The concept paper reframes it: the blueprint states "what happens"; the concept paper argues "why it should happen and why this approach fits the domain."
- **AI Validates, Not Generates**: The last 10% uses AI to find weaknesses in the paper, not to write it. Starting with AI produces a document the builder cannot defend, because the arguments are outsourced rather than understood.

### Critical Patterns

- The 10-80-10 ratio is a diagnostic: if the first segment needs 30%, the 80% (domain knowledge) is insufficient and the builder should return to Phase 1 exploration.
- Human research must precede AI validation because the builder needs enough domain understanding to evaluate whether the AI's feedback is correct and applicable. Without it, they cannot distinguish valid critique from generic advice.
- The AI validation loop continues until feedback shifts from substantive (gaps, contradictions, missing sections) to stylistic (reorder, rephrase, add a mention). That shift is the signal the paper is ready.

### Common Mistakes

- Starting with AI to "get research done faster" (produces polished generic answers the builder cannot evaluate against their specific context)
- Treating the AI validation step as "ask AI to write the paper" rather than "ask AI to attack the paper"
- Misreading the 10-80-10 ratio as arbitrary rather than as a diagnostic signal about domain readiness
