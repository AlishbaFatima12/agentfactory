# From Concept Paper to Domain Mastery

Emma came back with a fresh cup of coffee and pulled a chair next to James. "Show me where we are."

James pointed at the whiteboard where Emma's three-box diagram from Chapter 64 still sat:

```
[Blueprint]  →  [Concept Paper]  →  [Agent Skills]
  Ch 64            Ch 65              Ch 67
```

"We're here." He pointed at the second box. "Concept paper done. Three advisory board loops. Crystallization signal reached."

"Almost." Emma picked up the marker and added a box between the Concept Paper and Agent Skills:

```
[Blueprint]  →  [Concept Paper]  →  [Domain Mastery]  →  [Agent Skills]
  Ch 64            Ch 65              Ch 66                Ch 67
```

"There is a step between your concept paper and your agent skills. Can you guess what it tests?"

James thought about it. "Whether I actually understand the domain well enough to extract skills from it?"

"Exactly. The concept paper proves the idea is sound. The **Domain Mastery Gate** proves YOU have internalized the domain deeply enough to make decisions about it without consulting the paper every time."

## Why Domain Mastery Matters

"Here is the problem the gate solves," Emma said. "In Chapter 67, you will extract four agent skills from the concept paper. Each skill defines what one FTE knows and how it reasons. The Resume Screener's skill, for example, must encode scoring rubrics, edge case handling, and domain-specific judgment."

She turned to James. "Can you write a scoring rubric for a Senior Data Engineer without looking at the concept paper?"

James hesitated. "I could write a rough one. Years of experience, technical skills, relevant projects."

"What about career changers? Someone with five years in data science who is pivoting to data engineering?"

"That's a grey area. The concept paper addresses it, but I'd have to look it up."

"That is why the gate exists. If you have to look it up, you have not internalized it. And if you have not internalized it, your agent skill will be vague: 'evaluate candidates fairly.' A vague skill produces vague agent behavior. The factory breaks because the skill was written by someone who understood the structure but not the substance."

James nodded slowly. "Wait, so basically the concept paper is the document, and domain mastery is the knowledge in my head? Two different things?"

"A surgeon reads the procedure manual. That is the concept paper. A surgeon who can perform the procedure without the manual open. That is domain mastery. The gate tests whether you have crossed from the first to the second."

## Where the Concept Paper Goes Next

Emma drew arrows from the concept paper box to multiple downstream chapters:

```
                           ┌─→ Ch 67: Agent Skills (extract 4 skills)
                           │
[Concept Paper, Ch 65] ────┼─→ Ch 76: Database Schemas (data contracts become tables)
                           │
                           ├─→ Ch 78-81: FTE Build (each FTE grounded in concept paper)
                           │
                           └─→ Ch 90: Capstone (verify against concept paper)
```

"The concept paper is not a one-time artifact," she said. "It is the reference document for the entire Part 6 build. When you build the Resume Screener in Chapter 79, you will check its behavior against the concept paper's claims. When you design the database schema in Chapter 76, you will derive the tables from the concept paper's data contracts. When you assemble the full factory in Chapter 90, you will verify it against the concept paper's success criteria."

"So everything traces back to this document."

"Everything traces back to THIS document, which traces back to the blueprint, which traces back to the Domain Decomposition. The chain is unbroken."

## The Phase Transition

Emma circled the Domain Mastery Gate on the whiteboard. "This is the boundary between Phase 1 (Explore) and Phase 2 (Incubate) of the Agent Maturity Model."

| What You Have After Phase 1 | What You Build in Phase 2       |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Factory Blueprint (Ch 64)   | Agent skills for 4 FTEs (Ch 67) |
| Concept Paper (Ch 65)       | Simulation results (Ch 68)      |
| Domain Mastery (Ch 66)      | MCP servers (Ch 69-71)          |

"Phase 1 is thinking. Phase 2 is prototyping. The concept paper is the last thinking artifact. After Chapter 66, you start building intelligence." She capped the marker. "Not code. Not yet. Intelligence: the skills and protocols that define how each FTE reasons. Code comes in Phase 3."

James looked at the whiteboard. Four chapters of architecture (Ch 61-64). Two chapters of exploration (Ch 65-66). Then the build begins.

"I've been wanting to write code since Chapter 61," he said.

"And every chapter since then has been making sure the code you write is worth writing." Emma took a sip of her coffee. "You have a validated concept paper, a complete blueprint, and a clear understanding of four FTE roles. When you get to Chapter 67 and start extracting skills, you will know exactly what each skill needs to do. That is not a constraint on your speed. It is the reason your speed will compound."
