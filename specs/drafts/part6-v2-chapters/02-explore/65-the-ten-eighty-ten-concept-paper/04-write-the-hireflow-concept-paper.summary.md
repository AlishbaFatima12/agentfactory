### Core Concept

Lesson 4 is the applied exercise: James writes the HireFlow concept paper using the 10-80-10 structure, then runs three advisory board loops. The lesson demonstrates the complete method from blank template through crystallization, showing what the feedback classification process looks like in practice and how the paper tightens across loops.

### Key Mental Models

- **Template as Scaffold**: The six-section template (Problem Statement, Proposed Solution, Domain Evidence, FTE Justification, Risk Assessment, Success Criteria) provides the structure. The Factory Blueprint provides the content. Research findings go into Section 3. The template prevents blank-page paralysis.
- **FTE Justification Criteria in Practice**: An FTE must have Distinct Input, Distinct Expertise, and Independent Failure. The Interview Question Generator initially fails Distinct Input (it receives from two sources), but James resolves this by arguing that synthesis of two streams IS the distinct expertise. This is the right reasoning.
- **Loop Compression**: Loop 1 catches structural problems (scoring weights, logical contradiction, missing privacy section). Loop 2 catches medium issues (regional differences, bias scenarios). Loop 3 returns only stylistic feedback. Three loops is typical; four or more indicates thin domain knowledge.

### Critical Patterns

- James stalls on Section 3 (Domain Evidence) and almost messaged Emma for help. Emma had said to write without waiting. He forced himself to start with the easiest finding, and the rest followed. The friction at Section 3 is normal because it requires connecting research evidence to blueprint claims.
- The Logic Auditor caught a contradiction James would not have seen himself: "human review gates preserve judgment" vs. "FTEs fail independently." These are compatible but required explicit clarification in the paper.
- The Gap Finder identified data privacy as a missing section. Privacy was not in James's blueprint, but it is a real dependency for any system that processes candidate personal information.

### Common Mistakes

- Waiting for external review before submitting to the advisory board (the draft does not need to be perfect before the first loop; the loop is how it improves)
- Writing Section 3 last or skipping it (Domain Evidence is where blueprint assumptions get tested; skipping it defeats the purpose of the concept paper)
- Accepting advisory board scores at face value without checking whether high scores accompanied substantive or stylistic feedback
