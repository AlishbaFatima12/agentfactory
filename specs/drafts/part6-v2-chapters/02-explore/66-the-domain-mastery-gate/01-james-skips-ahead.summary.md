### Core Concept

A finished concept paper is not a license to build. James's HireFlow paper scored 9.6 on the quality rubric and covered all four FTE specifications in detail. Yet five rapid-fire questions from Emma exposed four domain gaps he did not know he had. The concept paper proves you understand the domain's structure. It does not prove you understand the domain's depth.

### Key Mental Models

- **Structure vs. depth**: A concept paper maps the happy path. Depth means knowing what to do when the map runs out: employment gaps, conflicting signals, compliance requirements, edge-case judgment calls.
- **Blind spot inheritance**: Gaps the principal does not recognize never get encoded as constraints in agent skills. The agent then operates as if those gaps do not exist, failing silently in exactly the situations the principal cannot catch.
- **The principal competence test**: Chapter 61 asked how to govern an agent. This lesson asks the prior question: are you competent enough to govern one? Reviewing agent output against a standard you do not fully understand is not review.

### Critical Patterns

- The concept paper quality rubric evaluates structure and coherence; it does not evaluate the author's ability to handle hard cases
- A blind spot is an unrecognized gap. A known limitation can be encoded ("escalate to human if X"). An unknown gap becomes silent failure.
- James's Resume Screener, if written from his current knowledge, would penalize every candidate with an employment gap because the skill would never address the scenario

### Common Mistakes

- Treating a high concept paper score as proof of domain mastery (the paper measures the document; the gate measures the author)
- Believing edge cases can be learned during construction (you cannot catch agent errors you cannot recognize)
- Assuming "I can learn as I build" is safe when agent failures are invisible to someone with the same gaps
