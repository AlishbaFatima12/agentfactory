# Take the HireFlow Domain Quiz

James had his concept paper open in one tab and his AI assistant in another. "I scored well on Emma's questions last time. Five for five on the fact questions."

"You scored well on five questions I chose," Emma said. "Five is not thirty. And I chose easy ones on purpose."

He looked at her. "You chose the easy ones?"

"The first round is always easy. It builds false confidence. The gate is designed to break that confidence in a controlled way, before your agents break it for you in production."

She pointed at his concept paper. "Your concept paper is your source material. Your AI assistant generates the quiz. You take it alone. No AI, no notes, no searching. Then you score yourself honestly and fix what you find."

Emma stood up. "I will be back in an hour. Do not skip a single step."

---

## Step 1: Generate Your Quiz

Open your AI assistant and paste the prompt below. Replace the placeholder with your actual concept paper text from Chapter 65.

```
I need you to generate a Domain Mastery Gate quiz based on my concept paper.

Here is my concept paper:

[PASTE YOUR FULL CONCEPT PAPER HERE]

Generate exactly 30 questions divided into three dimensions:

**Dimension 1: Fact Questions (10 questions)**
These test verifiable domain claims. Each question has one correct answer.
The answer should require domain understanding, not just reading comprehension.

**Dimension 2: Edge-Case Questions (10 questions)**
These test unusual situations that require domain judgment. Each scenario
should involve conflicting signals, incomplete information, or boundary
conditions that a recruitment professional would encounter.

**Dimension 3: Judgment-Call Questions (10 questions)**
These test situations with no single right answer. Each question should
present a dilemma where reasonable professionals might disagree. The
quality of reasoning matters more than the specific answer.

Quality criteria for all questions:
- Questions must require DOMAIN UNDERSTANDING, not surface recall
- Each question should test a different concept from the paper
- Edge cases and judgment calls should reference realistic scenarios
- Include enough context in each question for the reader to reason
  through an answer without external references

Format each question with its number, dimension label, and the full
question text. Do not include answers or hints.
```

Before you accept the generated quiz, check it for quality. A weak question tests recall of your paper's exact wording. A strong question tests whether you understand the domain well enough to apply, extend, or defend the claims in your paper.

**Weak question** (tests recall):

> "According to your concept paper, what are the four FTE roles in HireFlow?"

This is weak because a student could answer it by scanning the paper for a list. It does not test domain understanding.

**Strong question** (tests mastery):

> "A candidate's resume shows a two-year employment gap between their last two positions. Their cover letter does not address it. As the Resume Screener FTE, what signals would you look for elsewhere in the application to distinguish between a red flag and a neutral gap?"

This is strong because it requires understanding of recruitment judgment, not just recall of what the paper says about resume screening.

Read through all 30 questions. If more than five feel like recall questions, ask your AI assistant to regenerate the weak ones with this follow-up prompt:

```
Questions [list the numbers] are too easy. They test recall of my paper's
wording rather than domain understanding. Regenerate those questions at
mastery level. Each replacement should require me to APPLY domain knowledge
to a specific scenario, not recall what I wrote from memory.
```

## Step 2: Take the Quiz AI-Free

This step requires discipline. The entire value of the Domain Mastery Gate depends on honest, unassisted answers.

1. **Close your AI assistant.** Close the tab or window entirely. Do not minimize it.
2. **Close your concept paper.** You wrote it; you should know what is in it.
3. **Open a blank document.** Label it "Domain Mastery Gate: [Your Name], [Today's Date]."
4. **Set a timer for 60 minutes.** Most people finish in 45 minutes. If you need longer, take longer, but the timer keeps you from overthinking.
5. **Answer all 30 questions in order.** For fact questions, write a direct answer. For edge-case and judgment-call questions, write your reasoning, not just your conclusion. A bare answer like "I would reject the candidate" is not enough. Write the two or three factors that led you to that conclusion.

Do not skip questions. If you do not know the answer, write "I do not know" and move on. Blank answers and honest "I do not know" entries are the most valuable data the gate produces. They mark your blind spots precisely.

## Step 4: Create Your Remediation Plan

For every question you scored below mastery (0 or 1), fill in one row of the remediation plan. The critical column is "Source to Study." This must be a **primary source**: a textbook, industry regulation, professional standard, or domain-specific reference. Not an AI summary. Not a blog post. Not a Wikipedia article.

The reason for primary sources: AI summaries inherit the same blind spots you are trying to fix. If your gap is in anti-discrimination compliance, you need the actual regulation text, not a chatbot's paraphrase of it. The goal is to build understanding that is independent of any AI system.

| Gap Area                                | Dimension     | Source to Study                                   | Target Date |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ----------- |
| Example: employment gap evaluation      | Edge Case     | SHRM Talent Acquisition Guide, Chapter 4          | [date]      |
| Example: anti-discrimination compliance | Fact          | EEOC Compliance Manual, Section 15                | [date]      |
| Example: conflicting reference signals  | Judgment Call | "Who" by Geoff Smart, reference check methodology | [date]      |
|                                         |               |                                                   |             |
|                                         |               |                                                   |             |
|                                         |               |                                                   |             |

Set realistic target dates. Cramming through five primary sources in a single evening produces shallow recall, not deep understanding. Space your study across several days if you have more than three gaps.

---

## Step 5: Re-test

After completing your remediation study, return to your AI assistant and generate **new** questions. Do not re-take the original quiz. You already know those answers; re-taking them tests memory, not mastery.

Use this prompt:

```
I completed my Domain Mastery Gate quiz and identified gaps in these areas:

[LIST YOUR GAP AREAS FROM THE REMEDIATION PLAN]

Generate 5 new questions for each gap area, using the same three-dimension
format (facts, edge cases, judgment calls). These questions should test
whether I have closed the gap, not whether I remember the original questions.

Make the edge cases and judgment calls harder than the first round.
I have studied the primary sources and should be tested at a deeper level.
```

Take the re-test AI-free. Score it using the same rubric. If any dimension still falls below 70%, study the specific gap and re-test again. The loop continues until you clear the threshold.

## James Reports Back

James returned an hour later with his scoring sheet. Emma glanced at the totals.

"Seventy-eight percent," she said. It was not a question.

"Facts were fine. Ninety percent. But edge cases hit me at sixty-five percent, and judgment calls were seventy." He set the sheet down. "I thought I understood recruitment. I wrote a twenty-page concept paper about it."

"You understand the happy path. The standard resume, the cooperative candidate, the straightforward job description. Your concept paper reflects that understanding accurately."

"And the other thirty-five percent?"

"Employment gaps with no explanation. Candidates who interview well but whose references tell a different story. Anti-discrimination rules that interact with seemingly neutral screening criteria. Those are the situations your agents will encounter every day."

James stared at his remediation plan. Four rows. Two edge-case gaps, two judgment-call gaps.

"Wait, so basically the gate is protecting my future agents from my current ignorance?"

"That is exactly right. Every gap on that sheet is a place where your Resume Screener would make a bad call, your Interview Generator would ask the wrong question, or your Candidate Summarizer would miss a critical signal. Fix these before you write a single agent skill."

He picked up the sheet again. "I have the SHRM guide and the EEOC manual on my list. Actual sources, not AI summaries."

"Good. When you can answer edge cases in those areas without hesitating, you are ready for Chapter 67."

:::tip Key Insight
The Domain Mastery Gate is not a test you pass once and forget. Every time you enter a new domain, apply the same five steps: generate, take, score, remediate, re-test. The domain changes. The method does not. Your agents are only as reliable as the knowledge you bring to their supervision.
:::
