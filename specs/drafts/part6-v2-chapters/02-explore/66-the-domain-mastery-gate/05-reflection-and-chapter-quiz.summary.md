### Core Concept

Domain mastery gates exist because confidence is not competence. People with shallow knowledge in a subject tend to overestimate their readiness. The concept paper can feel thorough even when it covers only the happy path. The Dunning-Kruger defense is the gate's function: it surfaces blind spots in a controlled environment before they become encoded into agent skills and deployed against real candidates.

### Key Mental Models

- **Vague knowledge produces vague skills**: If your concept paper says "screen candidates for relevant experience," your skill will say the same vague thing. The agent interprets "relevant" from its training data. If your paper says "apply the two-year recency threshold from the hiring policy," that precision carries forward into the skill and into the agent's behavior.
- **The gate as a transferable pattern**: The five steps (Generate, Take, Score, Remediate, Re-test) are domain-independent. Passing the recruitment gate says nothing about finance competence. The method transfers; the knowledge does not.
- **Supervision requires comprehension**: Chapter 61 established that the principal must be competent enough to verify agent output. The gate builds that competence. A principal who cannot pass the gate cannot distinguish good agent output from bad output in the same domain.

### Critical Patterns

- The gate is not a one-time event. Any domain extension, new industry vertical, or regulatory change triggers a new pass through the five steps.
- The per-dimension threshold (no dimension below 70%) catches uneven preparation that a strong overall score would mask
- Primary sources matter for remediation because they provide independent ground truth; AI summaries may reproduce the same gaps the student is trying to close

### Common Mistakes

- Conflating the gate's method with domain-specific knowledge (passing the gate in one domain does not transfer to another domain)
- Treating a high overall score as a pass when a single dimension is below 70%
- Using AI to take the quiz instead of the student (measures AI capabilities, hides the student's blind spots)
