## GO-TO-MARKET WORKFLOW

### Task Types

TYPE 1: ICP DEFINITION
  Input: Problem area; discovery data; current customer data
  Output: Primary ICP (role, company, trigger, signals, NOT a fit) + secondary ICP

TYPE 2: POSITIONING STATEMENT
  Input: ICP; value proposition; competitive landscape
  Output: Positioning statement (For/Who/Our product/That/Unlike/We format)

TYPE 3: CHANNEL STRATEGY
  Input: ICP; team constraints; budget; existing assets (network, platform, brand)
  Output: Channels ranked by CAC efficiency; Week 1 activation for each

TYPE 4: SALES PROCESS
  Input: ICP; product type; sales cycle data
  Output: Step-by-step process from lead identification to signed contract

TYPE 5: 90-DAY GTM CALENDAR
  Input: Target (N customers by Day 90); channel strategy; team
  Output: Week-by-week activity plan with decision gates at Day 30 and Day 60

TYPE 6: PRICING STRATEGY
  Input: WTP data from discovery; competitive pricing; unit economics
  Output: Pricing tiers; anchoring strategy; discounting policy; trial policy

TYPE 7: CUSTOMER SUCCESS PROGRAMME
  Input: Target segment; onboarding complexity; churn risk factors
  Output: Day 1–90 success programme with adoption metrics and intervention triggers

### Positioning Statement Format

  For:         [Primary ICP — the specific person]
  Who:         [The problem they have — from discovery language]
  Our product: [Category name — what kind of thing it is]
  That:        [The primary value delivered — outcome, not feature]
  Unlike:      [The primary alternative they currently use]
  We:          [The specific differentiating claim — why you win]

Quality test: Read it to someone who knows your space.
  If they say "that sounds like [competitor]" → not differentiated enough
  If they say "I've never heard anyone say that" → potentially too different
  If they say "that makes sense; I can see why that matters" → good positioning

### Channel Strategy Framework

CHANNEL RANKING CRITERIA (rank each channel on all three):
  CAC efficiency: How much does it cost to acquire one customer through this channel?
  Scalability:    Can this channel grow without the founder's time?
  Speed:          How quickly can this channel produce results?

CHANNEL ARCHETYPES:

  FOUNDER-LED OUTREACH (early stage; months 1–6):
  CAC: Low (founder time) | Scale: Low | Speed: Fast
  Best for: ICP refinement; first 10 customers; reference case development
  Activate: Identify 100 ICP-matching prospects on LinkedIn; write personalised
  outreach citing their specific context (company + likely pain); aim for 10%
  meeting-to-close rate

  CONTENT / THOUGHT LEADERSHIP (months 3–12):
  CAC: Medium-low | Scale: Medium | Speed: Slow to start; compounds
  Best for: Building inbound pipeline over 6–12 months
  Activate: Write 1 long-form piece per week on the problem you solve;
  publish where your ICP spends time (LinkedIn; industry newsletter; podcast)

  COMMUNITY AND ASSOCIATIONS (months 3–9):
  CAC: Medium | Scale: Medium | Speed: Medium
  Best for: Warm introductions in trust-based markets
  Activate: Join 2–3 associations where your ICP is active; contribute before
  selling; speak at one event in Month 3–6

  REFERRAL PROGRAMME (months 6+):
  CAC: Low | Scale: Medium | Speed: Depends on customer base
  Best for: Once you have 10+ happy customers who know others with the problem
  Activate: Ask your top 3 customers for introductions; formalise with incentive

  INSIDE SALES (months 9–18; requires funding):
  CAC: Medium-high | Scale: High | Speed: Fast once ramped
  Best for: Scaling what founder-led sales has proven; not for finding PMF
  Activate: Hire 1 SDR; give them the playbook founder-led sales built; measure
  conversion at each stage; ramp to quota over 90 days

  PARTNERSHIPS / CHANNELS (months 12+):
  CAC: Variable | Scale: High | Speed: Slow to develop
  Best for: Distribution leverage — selling through partners who already
  have your ICP as customers
  Activate: Identify 3 non-competing partners who serve your ICP; propose
  a simple referral arrangement; test conversion before formalising

### Pricing Strategy Rules

ANCHOR HIGH, JUSTIFY, THEN TIER:
  Start with the highest price you can credibly defend.
  Add a lower tier for smaller customers or a stripped-down version.
  Never start low — it is very hard to raise prices; easier to add tiers.

VALUE-BASED PRICING PRINCIPLE:
  Price should be <10% of the value delivered (the "10% rule").
  If you save a customer $50,000/year, you can charge up to $5,000/year
  and still have a compelling ROI story.

DISCOUNTING POLICY:
  Annual prepay only: 1–2 months free for annual commitment.
  No arbitrary discounts: discounts train customers to wait;
  customers who price-negotiate before signing churn faster.

TRIAL POLICY:
  Paid trial preferred over free trial: even a nominal amount signals commitment.
  Free trial only for high-value strategic accounts where relationship matters.
  Trial with LOI preferred: require a letter of intent before granting trial access.

## NEVER DO THESE

- NEVER define ICP as a demographic without a buying trigger —
  "CFOs at $10M companies" is a description; "CFOs at $10M companies
  who just had an audit finding" is an ICP with urgency
- NEVER launch multiple channels simultaneously without measuring each —
  start with one; add the second only when the first is producing
  measurable results
- NEVER set pricing based on competitor pricing alone — price based on
  value delivered (WTP from discovery) and unit economics (CAC payback)
- NEVER skip the "NOT a fit" section of the ICP — knowing who not to
  sell to is as valuable as knowing who to sell to
- NEVER design a customer success programme that assumes high-touch
  is sustainable — design for the support model you will have at
  50 customers, not the one you have today with 5
