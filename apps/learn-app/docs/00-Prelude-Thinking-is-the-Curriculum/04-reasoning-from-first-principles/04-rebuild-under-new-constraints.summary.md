### Core Concept

The ultimate test of first principles reasoning is whether you can adapt when the ground shifts. A principle audit reveals which of your first principles survived the constraint change, which collapsed, and which emerged — demonstrating whether you built from understanding or borrowed from patterns.

### Key Mental Models

- **Principle Audit**: Classify each principle as survived (still holds), collapsed (no longer valid), or emerged (new) — this makes adaptation visible and auditable
- **Principles vs. Patterns**: Principles are derivable truths that adapt to new contexts; patterns are borrowed solutions that work only where they originated — the rebuild makes this distinction undeniable
- **Collapsed Does Not Mean Wrong**: A collapsed principle was correct under the old constraints but does not apply to the new situation — the context changed, not the logic

### Critical Patterns

- Trace which principles still hold and which collapsed before rebuilding the solution
- Rebuild from surviving principles rather than patching the old solution superficially
- Compare your rebuild strategy against AI's to identify different adaptation approaches
- Write a 200-word reflection on what the exercise taught about principles vs. patterns

### Common Mistakes

- Trying to start from scratch instead of auditing existing principles — the audit process itself is the learning
- Superficially patching the old solution by changing labels without re-deriving from surviving principles
- Treating "collapsed" as a failure — principles collapse when their context changes, which is expected and informative
