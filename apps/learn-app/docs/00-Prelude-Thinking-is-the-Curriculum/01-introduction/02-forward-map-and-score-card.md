# Forward Map and Score Card

## How the Ten Chapters Connect: A Forward Map

The ten chapters are not ten independent workshops. Each one produces a skill or tool that later chapters depend on. Before you begin, here is the forward map showing what each chapter teaches and exactly where that skill returns:

| Chapter                                   | Core Skill                                                               | Where It Goes Next                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ch 1: Asking Better Questions**         | Prediction Lock format, Reasoning Receipt format, questioning discipline | Prediction Lock returns in Ch 2, 4, 7, 9. Reasoning Receipt becomes primary documentation in Ch 5, 6, 8, 9. Questioning discipline applies to every AI interaction for the rest of this part.                                                                                                                                       |
| **Ch 2: Detecting Broken Reasoning**      | Error Taxonomy, Confidence Calibration                                   | Error Taxonomy used to annotate AI output in Ch 3, 5, 6, 9. Confidence Calibration returns explicitly in Ch 9 and implicitly throughout. Error detection becomes your override trigger in Ch 6.                                                                                                                                     |
| **Ch 3: Thinking in Systems**             | Cascade Map technique                                                    | Applies in Ch 7 (tracing ethical consequences across stakeholders), Ch 8 (anticipating downstream effects of creative designs), Ch 9 (understanding how decisions cascade through uncertain environments). Systems thinking becomes the lens for all complex problems.                                                              |
| **Ch 4: Reasoning From First Principles** | Assumption Autopsy, Rebuild Under New Constraints                        | Assumption Autopsy returns in Ch 8 (testing creative work for hidden assumptions) and Ch 9 (identifying assumptions in incomplete data). Rebuild Under Constraints is mirrored in Ch 7 and Ch 9. First principles reasoning is what you use when no existing pattern applies.                                                       |
| **Ch 5: Communicating What Matters**      | Audience analysis, Live adaptation                                       | Audience analysis applies in Ch 7 (stakeholders in ethical dilemmas), Ch 10 (teaching back to peers), and throughout the rest of the book when explaining technical decisions to non-technical audiences. Live adaptation is the same muscle used in Ch 7 and Ch 10.                                                                |
| **Ch 6: Working With AI, Not For AI**     | Collaboration Log format, Override instinct, Three-path comparison       | Collaboration Log becomes your tool for AI interaction in Ch 8, 9, 10. Override instinct prevents accepting bad AI output in every future exercise. Three-path comparison (solo vs. pure AI vs. collaboration) establishes the mental model for Parts 2-10.                                                                         |
| **Ch 7: Reasoning Through Dilemmas**      | Stakeholder Cost Matrix, Adversarial defence                             | Stakeholder Cost Matrix applies whenever you build AI systems affecting real people (every project in Parts 5-10). Adversarial defence prepares you for Ch 9 and for defending technical decisions throughout the rest of the book.                                                                                                 |
| **Ch 8: Building Something From Nothing** | Creation Log, Three-draft evolution, Originality Test                    | Creation Log and three-draft evolution become your default workflow for every original deliverable in the rest of the book. The Originality Test ("could AI have produced this?") becomes a self-check for any work product in Parts 2-10.                                                                                          |
| **Ch 9: Deciding Under Uncertainty**      | Reversal Trigger, Decision Audit                                         | Reversal Trigger applies to every architectural, design, and strategic decision in Parts 2-10. Decision Audit (retrospectively analyzing your own decisions) becomes the meta-cognitive skill you use after every major project. Deciding with incomplete information is the most transferable skill to the building-focused parts. |
| **Ch 10: Learning How to Learn**          | Personal Learning Framework                                              | This framework is your operating system for Parts 2-10. Every new technology, framework, SDK, and deployment pattern is an unfamiliar domain. Agent architectures, cloud-native systems, LLMOps, real-time voice agents -- Chapter 10's meta-learning strategy is how you navigate all of them.                                     |

This forward map is not decorative. It is a promise: nothing you learn in Part 0 is theoretical. Every skill, every tool, every framework developed across these ten chapters will be called upon -- by name -- in the parts that follow. When you build your first AI agent in Part 5 and need to decide between two architectures with incomplete information, you will use Chapter 9's Reversal Triggers. When you deploy a system that affects real users and must defend your design choices, you will use Chapter 7's Stakeholder Cost Matrix and Chapter 1's question discipline. Part 0 is not a warm-up. It is the foundation that makes everything else possible.

## The Thinking Score Card: A Unified Scoring System

Every AI check prompt in this part ends with the same standardized scoring request: the Thinking Score Card. This gives you five consistent scores (each 1-10) across all 40 exercises, all 10 chapters, allowing you to track your growth on a single chart from Chapter 1, Exercise 1 to Chapter 10, Exercise 4.

:::note The Thinking Score Card
Five dimensions, scored 1-10, on every exercise:

1. **Independent Thinking (1-10):** Did the student produce genuine thought before or beyond AI? Evidence of predictions, original analysis, or ideas that AI would not generate independently.
2. **Critical Evaluation (1-10):** Did the student evaluate AI output critically? Evidence of accepting, rejecting, or modifying AI responses with justified reasoning rather than passive acceptance.
3. **Reasoning Depth (1-10):** How deep is the student's reasoning? Evidence of second-order thinking, tracing causes to consequences, identifying hidden assumptions, and building logical chains rather than surface-level responses.
4. **Originality (1-10):** How much of the student's work diverges from what AI would produce if given the same prompt? Evidence of unique perspectives, novel connections, or creative approaches that go beyond standard AI output.
5. **Self-Awareness (1-10):** Does the student accurately understand their own strengths, weaknesses, and confidence levels? Evidence of calibrated confidence, honest gap identification, and reflections that match the quality of the work.
   :::

Every AI check prompt in every exercise ends with: "Finally, complete the Thinking Score Card for this exercise: Independent Thinking (1-10), Critical Evaluation (1-10), Reasoning Depth (1-10), Originality (1-10), Self-Awareness (1-10)." These five numbers are your trackable signal across the entire part.
