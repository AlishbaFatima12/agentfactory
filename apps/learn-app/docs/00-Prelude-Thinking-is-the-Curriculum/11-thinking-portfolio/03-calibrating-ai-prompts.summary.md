### Core Concept

AI prompts need semester-by-semester calibration because AI models evolve. The five-step Semester Calibration Protocol catches systematic scoring bias while keeping the five Thinking Score Card dimensions permanent.

### Key Mental Models

- **Calibrate the Instrument, Not the Measurement**: The five Score Card dimensions define what thinking means and must never change. Only the prompt wording that elicits accurate scores is tunable.
- **Systematic Bias vs. Random Noise**: Small per-exercise inaccuracies wash out over 40 data points. Systematic drift (inflation, compression, blind spots) does not -- and that is what calibration catches.

### Critical Patterns

- Score Distribution Audit: flag if 80%+ score 8+ by Chapter 3 (too lenient) or 50%+ below 4 by Chapter 8 (too harsh)
- Prompt Spot-Test: submit 5 archived deliverables (1 strong, 1 weak, 3 average) to current AI, compare to instructor assessment
- Feedback Challenge Review: if 30%+ challenges succeed, tighten prompts; if 0% challenge, students may be too deferential
- Model Migration: re-run spot-test before each semester when major AI model versions release
- Scenario Refresh: replace dated scenarios annually while keeping exercise structure and prompts unchanged

### Common Mistakes

- Attempting perfect AI scoring instead of aiming for consistent scoring that reliably distinguishes strong from weak thinking
- Changing Score Card dimensions when scores drift instead of adjusting prompt wording
- Skipping calibration because "it seemed fine last semester" -- AI models change between semesters
