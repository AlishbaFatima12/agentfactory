### Core Concept
When contradictory information arrives under time pressure, most people either anchor (refuse to change) or overreact (abandon everything). The skill is proportional updating — changing exactly as much as the evidence warrants, no more and no less.

### Key Mental Models
- **Three Response Patterns**: Anchoring (ignoring new evidence), overreaction (discarding all prior analysis), and proportional updating (revising only what the new evidence actually invalidates). Most people default to the first two.
- **Reversal Trigger Testing**: The Information Drop is where your Reversal Trigger from Exercise 1 gets tested against reality. If the new information matches your trigger condition, you should consider reversing — but with judgment, not automatically.

### Critical Patterns
- Track confidence calibration across three stages (original, post-consultation, post-drop) to reveal your pattern
- Ask whether the new information actually invalidates your reasoning or just introduces noise
- Use the Process Document to examine your own reaction: did you anchor, overreact, or update proportionally?
- Compare your Reversal Trigger against the new information explicitly

### Common Mistakes
- Treating the 20-minute time limit as unfair rather than realistic — real decisions almost always have time constraints
- Completely abandoning a position when new information contradicts one aspect of it (overreaction)
- Refusing to update at all because admitting you were wrong feels like failure (anchoring)
