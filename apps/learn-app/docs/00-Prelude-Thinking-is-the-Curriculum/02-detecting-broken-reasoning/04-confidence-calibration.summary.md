### Core Concept
Most people are systematically overconfident about AI accuracy. By quantifying your confidence calibration — rating 20 claims under time pressure and comparing against verified reality — you get a precise map of where your trust in AI is well-placed and where it is dangerous.

### Key Mental Models
- **Calibration Over Accuracy**: Knowing what you do not know is more valuable than being right — a well-calibrated person assigns high confidence only to claims they can verify, and low confidence to claims in unfamiliar territory
- **Time-Pressured Assessment**: Real-world AI use involves rapid judgment, not unlimited verification — the 90-second constraint trains the speed of assessment that actual decision-making requires

### Critical Patterns
- Rate each of 20 AI claims on a 0-100% confidence scale within 90 seconds
- Verify each claim afterward using AI and web research
- Classify each assessment as calibrated, overconfident, or underconfident
- Build a Confidence Calibration Chart plotting confidence ratings against verified accuracy
- Write a 200-word reflection analyzing calibration patterns by topic and claim type

### Common Mistakes
- Treating the goal as getting all 20 claims right rather than having calibrated confidence — high scores on wrong claims are worse than low scores on right claims
- Assuming 90 seconds is not enough time — it is sufficient to read carefully and notice red flags, but not enough to verify everything (which is the point)
- Ignoring topic-specific patterns in overconfidence — most students are overconfident in specific domains, not uniformly across all topics
