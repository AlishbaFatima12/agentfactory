### Core Concept

Ethical reasoning requires taking a clear position on dilemmas where values conflict, defending it with evidence, and honestly mapping who benefits and who bears the cost -- something AI refuses to do because it defaults to balanced, non-committal answers.

### Key Mental Models

- **Position Before Input**: Committing your stance in writing before consulting AI prevents your reasoning from being shaped by AI's diplomatic framing -- the sealed prediction lock proves independent thought
- **Stakeholder Cost Matrix**: Every ethical decision has winners and losers. Mapping all affected groups (not just sympathetic ones) with impact magnitude reveals the true cost of your position
- **Calibrated Confidence**: Pairing a confidence percentage with a specific reversal trigger separates genuine conviction from stubbornness

### Critical Patterns

- Seal your position with a timestamp before any AI interaction
- Write three distinct arguments with evidence, not just opinions
- List every stakeholder group including those you do not favor
- Specify a concrete, testable condition that would change your mind

### Common Mistakes

- Treating the exercise as finding the "right answer" when ethical dilemmas deliberately have no single correct position
- Listing only stakeholder groups you sympathize with while ignoring uncomfortable impacts
- Setting confidence at 95% with weak arguments, or 20% with strong ones -- calibration means matching confidence to evidence
