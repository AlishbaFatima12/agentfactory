### Core Concept
Arguing the opposite of your personal position is the highest test of ethical reasoning. If you can build the best case for the other side, you understand the dilemma deeply enough to hold any position responsibly.

### Key Mental Models
- **Perspective Reversal as Understanding Test**: The ability to argue convincingly for a view you disagree with proves deep comprehension -- surface understanding collapses under the pressure of live presentation
- **Empathy vs Agreement**: Understanding why someone holds a position is different from agreeing with it. The Stakeholder Swap separates intellectual depth from personal preference

### Critical Patterns
- Use AI to help prepare arguments for the opposite position, but document what you used
- Deliver a 5-minute live presentation with no AI access
- Peers rate argument strength, apparent conviction, and empathy separately
- Write a 200-word reflection on whether the exercise changed your understanding

### Common Mistakes
- Preparing surface-level arguments without understanding the values that drive the opposing position
- Confusing the exercise with dishonesty -- it tests depth of understanding, not willingness to lie
- Delivering arguments mechanically without apparent conviction, which reveals incomplete perspective-taking
