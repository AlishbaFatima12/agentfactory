### Core Concept

Deciding under uncertainty is a skill, not a guess. The quality of your decision comes from calibrated confidence, ranked information gaps, and specific reversal triggers — not from having all the data.

### Key Mental Models

- **Reversal Trigger**: Before committing to a decision, define the specific, testable condition that would make you change your mind. "If the market shifts" is vague; "if competitor pricing is below $50/month" is actionable.
- **Confidence Calibration**: Your confidence percentage should reflect how much you actually know, not how much you want to be right. A well-calibrated 55% is more valuable than an overconfident 90%.
- **Impact-Ranked Gaps**: There is always infinite missing information. The skill is identifying the 3 pieces that would most change your decision, ranked by impact.

### Critical Patterns

- Commit your decision in writing before consulting AI (the Prediction Lock format from Chapter 1, now applied to decisions)
- State a single clear recommendation, not a hedged list of options
- Define reversal triggers that are specific enough to test against real evidence
- Rank missing information by decision impact, not by category

### Common Mistakes

- Writing vague reversal triggers ("I'd change if things get worse") instead of testable ones ("I'd change if Q3 revenue drops below $2M")
- Treating low confidence as a sign of a bad decision — sometimes 55% is the best anyone can do with the data available
- Listing missing information by category (more market data, more customer data) instead of by how much it would change the decision
