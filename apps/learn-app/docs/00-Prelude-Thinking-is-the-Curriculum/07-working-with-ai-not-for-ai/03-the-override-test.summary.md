### Core Concept
The override instinct is the willingness and ability to say "the AI is wrong here" and back it up. Most students either trust AI too much (never override) or override randomly (reject good output). This exercise trains knowing WHEN and WHY to override.

### Key Mental Models
- **Override Instinct**: Targeted, justified correction of specific AI errors -- not blanket acceptance or blanket rejection, but strategic judgment about when AI output needs human correction
- **Prevention Over Detection**: Redesigning prompts to prevent error types is more valuable than spotting individual errors -- it creates a transferable skill rather than a one-time fix

### Critical Patterns
- Use AI to produce a competitive analysis, then find the deliberately embedded subtle error without being told what to look for
- Identify the error, explain why AI made it (error type and root cause), produce a corrected analysis, and redesign the prompt to prevent the error
- Document your error-detection process -- was it systematic or lucky?

### Common Mistakes
- Expecting the error to be obvious -- real AI errors in business analysis are subtle (logical flaws, outdated assumptions, false correlations)
- Skipping the prompt redesign step -- detection is a one-time fix, but redesigning prompts teaches prevention
- Overriding without justification, which is as problematic as accepting without evaluation
