### Core Concept

The quality of your questions determines the quality of every answer you get — from AI, colleagues, or data. The Prediction Lock forces you to commit your thinking in writing before touching AI, creating a measurable baseline that reveals where your reasoning is strong and where it is lazy.

### Key Mental Models

- **Diagnostic Power**: A question's value is measured by how many competing explanations it eliminates, not by how interesting or important it sounds
- **Predict-Then-Compare**: Writing predictions before seeing answers creates a feedback loop that exposes blind spots invisible to self-reflection alone
- **Critical Engagement Spectrum**: Every AI response demands a deliberate decision — accept, reject, or modify — with a justification that proves you thought about it

### Critical Patterns

- Seal your prediction document with a timestamp before opening any AI tool
- Rank questions by diagnostic power, not by topic importance
- Document every AI interaction in a Reasoning Receipt: prompt sent, response received, decision made, justification written
- Feed the same questions to both Claude and ChatGPT to compare outputs

### Common Mistakes

- Treating the Reasoning Receipt as bureaucratic paperwork rather than a mirror that reveals whether you actually thought or just copied
- Ranking questions by perceived importance rather than by their power to distinguish between competing explanations
- Accepting every AI response without critical evaluation — a pattern that produces a receipt full of "Accept" with no evidence of independent judgment
