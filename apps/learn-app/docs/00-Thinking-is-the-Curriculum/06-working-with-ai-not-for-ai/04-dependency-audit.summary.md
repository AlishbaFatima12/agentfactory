### Core Concept

Disagreement between AI tools is one of the most valuable signals you can get -- it means the question has genuine complexity and requires human judgment to resolve. The arbitration skill (evaluating, synthesizing, and improving) is the core of AI collaboration done well.

### Key Mental Models

- **AI Disagreement as Signal**: When AI tools disagree, the question has genuine complexity. Agreement might mean the answer is easy or both tools share the same blind spot
- **Synthesis vs. Averaging**: True synthesis creates something better than either input by selecting the strongest elements from each and adding original human judgment. Averaging just takes half from each and dilutes both

### Critical Patterns

- Ask Claude and ChatGPT the same strategic question and receive two different recommendations
- Evaluate each recommendation's strengths and weaknesses, then declare a verdict on which is stronger
- Synthesize a superior third option with clear attribution showing which elements came from each AI and which from your own judgment

### Common Mistakes

- Taking half from each recommendation (averaging) instead of evaluating each element and creating something genuinely better (synthesis)
- Assuming one AI tool is always better -- different tools have different strengths on different questions
- Avoiding a clear verdict in favor of diplomatic "both have merit" -- the arbitration requires a justified position
