### Core Concept

The most dangerous student is not the one who ignores AI -- it is the one who trusts it completely. This exercise builds the judgment layer between receiving AI output and acting on it through direct comparison of three approaches: solo, pure AI, and genuine collaboration.

### Key Mental Models

- **Three-Path Comparison**: Solving the same problem three ways (solo, pure AI, collaboration) reveals through direct evidence what AI adds and what human judgment adds
- **Collaboration vs. Delegation**: Genuine collaboration means prompting, evaluating, overriding, and iterating -- not just accepting AI output with minor edits
- **Pivot from Solo to Collaborative Thinking**: Chapters 1-5 built solo thinking skills; this chapter applies them as the judgment layer in AI collaboration

### Critical Patterns

- Solve one complex problem three ways under strict time limits (45 min solo, 20 min pure AI, 30 min collaboration)
- Submit all three solutions with timestamps proving compliance with time limits
- Write a 300-400 word comparison analysis identifying where each approach was strongest and where human judgment specifically added value

### Common Mistakes

- Expecting the solo version to always be worst -- sometimes solo thinking captures original insights that AI collaboration channels away
- Treating collaboration as "use AI and edit the output" instead of genuine prompting, evaluating, modifying, and overriding
- Assuming the collaboration version is automatically best -- the AI Check may reveal that some interventions made elements worse
