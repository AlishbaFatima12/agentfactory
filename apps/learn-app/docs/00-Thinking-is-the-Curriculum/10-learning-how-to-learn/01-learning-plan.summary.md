### Core Concept
Learning itself requires a strategy. The Learning Plan is a Prediction Lock about your own learning process — committed before you start, then tested against reality during the 72-hour sprint and evaluated in the retrospective.

### Key Mental Models
- **Learning Plan as Prediction Lock**: Just as Chapter 1 committed a diagnosis before AI, this exercise commits a learning strategy before studying. The value comes from comparing plan to reality afterward.
- **Skip Decision Framework**: Explicit decisions about what NOT to learn are as important as what to learn. Time is finite; trying to learn everything means mastering nothing.
- **Testable Competence**: Define "enough" as what you can demonstrably do ("analyze a drug interaction and identify three risks"), not what you vaguely know ("understand pharmacology").

### Critical Patterns
- Design a phased time allocation that front-loads foundations rather than spreading time equally
- Choose resources appropriate for a complete beginner, not intermediate references
- Define a testable competence threshold calibrated to 72 hours
- Include an AI usage plan: when AI helps learning vs. when it hinders it
- Predict your biggest challenge before starting

### Common Mistakes
- Defining "enough" as "know everything" instead of a realistic, testable threshold for the available time
- Allocating equal time to all phases instead of front-loading foundational concepts
- Skipping the "what to skip" decision, which leads to unfocused, shallow learning
- Treating the plan as a rigid schedule rather than a prediction to be tested
