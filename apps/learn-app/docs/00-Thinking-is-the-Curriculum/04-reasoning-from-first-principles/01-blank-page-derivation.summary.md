### Core Concept
First principles reasoning strips a problem to its base truths and rebuilds from there, producing original solutions rather than borrowed patterns. The key distinction is between deriving conclusions from constraints (principles) vs. citing what worked or failed elsewhere (analogy/examples).

### Key Mental Models
- **Principles vs. Analogy**: "In markets where trust is primary, MVP destroys credibility" (principle) vs. "Company X tried MVP and failed" (example); principles predict new failures, examples only catalog past ones
- **Base Constraints**: Facts true regardless of approach; the non-negotiable truths from which any valid solution must be derived
- **Independent Derivation**: Writing without AI first reveals what you can reason through independently, creating a measurable baseline before comparison

### Critical Patterns
- Write the full 500-word contrarian argument without any AI or reference materials
- Identify at least 3 specific conditions under which the best practice fails
- State the first principles explicitly; the constraint-based reasoning leading to each conclusion
- After submitting, compare against AI feedback to distinguish principle-based from example-based reasoning

### Common Mistakes
- Treating "contrarian" as "contrary for its own sake"; the goal is identifying genuine failure conditions, not reflexive disagreement
- Listing companies where the approach failed (examples) instead of explaining structural conditions that cause failure (principles)
- Assuming that writing without AI produces worse work; the point is to discover what you can derive independently
