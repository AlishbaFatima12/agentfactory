### Core Concept
Solving a novel problem with no established solution forces pure first principles reasoning; identifying base constraints, listing assumptions explicitly, and deriving a solution through a logical chain. Comparing your derivation against AI's reveals whether you produced genuine independent reasoning or unconsciously borrowed familiar patterns.

### Key Mental Models
- **Constraints vs. Assumptions**: Constraints are true regardless of approach (limited supply, unequal need); assumptions are choices you make (equal access is fairest); confusing them produces fragile solutions
- **Derivation Chain**: "From constraint X + assumption Y, it follows that Z"; making reasoning visible and auditable, so others (and AI) can evaluate the logic, not just the conclusion
- **Divergence as Originality Signal**: Students reasoning from principles produce divergent solutions; those borrowing patterns converge; the spread of answers reveals the depth of independent thinking

### Critical Patterns
- Work for 45 minutes with no AI, no internet, no reference materials; derive from the problem statement alone
- Identify base constraints first (facts true regardless of approach), then list every assumption explicitly
- Document the derivation chain: constraint + assumption leads to requirement leads to solution design
- After submitting, ask AI to solve the same problem and compare where approaches converge and diverge

### Common Mistakes
- Confusing constraints with assumptions; constraints are non-negotiable facts, assumptions are choices that could be different
- Jumping to a solution without documenting the derivation chain; the chain is the deliverable, not the solution
- Thinking "novel problem" means no good answer exists; many valid solutions exist, each derived from different constraint prioritizations
