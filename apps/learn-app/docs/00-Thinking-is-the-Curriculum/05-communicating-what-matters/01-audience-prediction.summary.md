### Core Concept

Effective communication starts with audience modeling, not writing. Predicting what stakeholders care about, their likely objections, and the best persuasion strategy is the human skill that sits on top of any AI-generated draft.

### Key Mental Models

- **Audience-First Communication**: The same decision requires fundamentally different framing for different stakeholders -- communication is about the receiver, not the sender
- **Prediction-Then-Compare**: Write audience profiles before AI generates briefs, then compare to reveal where human judgment adds value AI cannot replicate
- **Communication Errors vs. Factual Errors**: Chapter 2's Error Taxonomy applied to communication -- detecting wrong emphasis, audience mismatch, and buried leads rather than logical flaws

### Critical Patterns

- Create three distinct audience profiles (priorities, predicted objection, persuasion strategy) before touching AI
- Generate AI persuasive briefs for each audience, then write a comparison document identifying where your human reading was more accurate
- Focus on political dynamics, emotional undercurrents, and organizational context -- the dimensions where human judgment consistently outperforms AI

### Common Mistakes

- Treating all stakeholders the same way instead of adapting the message for each audience's specific priorities
- Assuming AI audience analysis is always correct -- the exercise reveals specific dimensions where human insight outperforms AI
- Confusing good writing with good communication -- a polished brief can still fail if it misreads the audience
