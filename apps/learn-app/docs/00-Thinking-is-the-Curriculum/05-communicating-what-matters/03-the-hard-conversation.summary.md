### Core Concept
Strategic communication diagnosis means identifying why a message will fail to achieve its purpose -- not whether it is well-written. The skill is seeing tone, emphasis, and audience mismatch as errors with predictable behavioral consequences.

### Key Mental Models
- **Strategic vs. Grammatical Diagnosis**: A grammatically perfect email can be strategically disastrous -- the problems are in emphasis, audience mismatch, and buried lead, not in spelling or syntax
- **Behavioral Outcome Prediction**: The key diagnostic question is "What will the recipient DO?" not "How will the recipient FEEL?" -- behavior is the measurable consequence of communication

### Critical Patterns
- Diagnose strategic problems BEFORE rewriting -- the diagnosis document is half the deliverable
- Identify what the email is optimizing for that it should not be (wrong audience model, wrong emphasis)
- Explain every change in the rewrite with its strategic purpose -- surface edits without strategic reasoning miss the point

### Common Mistakes
- Focusing on grammar and polish when the real problems are strategic -- a polished email can still fail its purpose
- Thinking "rewriting" means making the email nicer -- sometimes the problem is that the email is too soft, not too harsh
- Skipping the diagnosis and going straight to rewriting -- without diagnosis, rewrites fix symptoms rather than causes
