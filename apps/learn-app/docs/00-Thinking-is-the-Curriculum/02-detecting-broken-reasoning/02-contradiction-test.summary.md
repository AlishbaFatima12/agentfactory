### Core Concept
When two confident AI responses disagree, the disagreement is a signal to think harder, not a reason to pick one randomly. Building a third analysis that improves on both forces genuine thinking, and the three-draft evolution reveals whether you can integrate feedback and grow or whether you stop after the first attempt.

### Key Mental Models
- **Contradiction as Signal**: AI disagreement reveals genuine complexity or uncertainty in a topic; investigating the divergence produces deeper understanding than either AI response alone
- **Evidence vs. Assertion**: Claims backed by data or reasoning are fundamentally different from claims stated confidently without support; learning to distinguish them is the core analytical skill

### Critical Patterns
- Ask both Claude and ChatGPT the same nuanced question where reasonable people disagree
- Identify and annotate every divergence point: "Claude claims X, ChatGPT claims Y; the evidence favors..."
- Write three progressively improved drafts with evolution notes explaining what changed and why
- Submit Draft 1 to AI for critique before writing Draft 2

### Common Mistakes
- Treating the goal as determining which AI is "right"; often both are partially right and partially wrong in different ways
- Making only cosmetic changes between drafts rather than substantive improvements driven by genuine engagement with critique
- Assuming that when both AI tools agree on a point, it must be correct; shared training data can produce shared blind spots
