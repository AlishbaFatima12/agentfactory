### Core Concept

Domain expertise is your most powerful error detection tool. In your own field, you catch errors AI gets subtly wrong that outsiders would accept. In an unfamiliar field, you discover how much harder error detection becomes; teaching you to seek expert review when the stakes are high.

### Key Mental Models

- **Expert Advantage**: Domain knowledge reveals errors invisible to non-experts . AI can sound plausible about any topic, but only someone with real knowledge can catch the subtle mistakes
- **Detection Rate Gap**: The difference between your error detection in a familiar vs. unfamiliar domain is a direct measure of how much domain expertise matters; and how cautious you should be outside your expertise

### Critical Patterns

- Generate AI analysis of a topic in your expert domain and annotate errors line-by-line using the Error Taxonomy
- Separate errors into two categories: those caught by expertise (non-expert would miss) and those suspected but unconfirmable
- Exchange annotated outputs with a partner from a different domain and attempt cross-verification
- Write a 200-word reflection comparing detection rates and articulating what this means for AI use

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming your partner's annotations are wrong because you cannot verify them; inability to verify reflects your knowledge gap, not annotation accuracy
- Thinking domain expertise only catches factual errors; experts also catch logical gaps, missing context, and cultural blind spots specific to their field
- Completing the exercise without changing how you actually use AI in unfamiliar domains
