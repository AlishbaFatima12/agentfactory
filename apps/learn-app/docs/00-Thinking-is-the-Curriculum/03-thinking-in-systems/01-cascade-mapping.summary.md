### Core Concept

A cascade map traces the rippling consequences of a single decision across multiple domains, revealing feedback loops and higher-order effects that linear analysis misses. The act of mapping before AI builds the mental habit of asking "and then what?"

### Key Mental Models

- **Linear vs. Systems Analysis**: AI lists effects in isolated categories; systems thinking traces connections between categories, especially circular feedback loops
- **Effect Orders**: First-order effects are obvious; second and third-order effects are where the real consequences hide; each step removed from the decision is harder to predict but often more impactful
- **Amplifying vs. Dampening Loops**: Feedback loops either reinforce the original effect (amplifying) or counteract it (dampening); identifying which type determines whether a decision's consequences grow or stabilize

### Critical Patterns

- Map effects across at least five domains (employees, customers, competitors, regulators, internal knowledge) before consulting AI
- For every effect, ask "and then what?" to push beyond first-order consequences
- Label each feedback loop with its mechanism; explain WHY the effect circles back, not just that it does
- Compare your human-generated map against AI output to discover complementary blind spots

### Common Mistakes

- Listing effects in each domain independently without drawing connections between domains; the map's value is in the connections, not the lists
- Confusing correlation with causation in causal chains; each arrow needs a mechanism explaining why A causes B
- Assuming more effects equals a better map; mechanism quality and genuine feedback loops matter more than quantity
