### Core Concept
Peer cross-examination tests whether you understand your cascade map deeply enough to explain and defend every connection without AI assistance. Peers reveal blind spots that AI cannot find because humans approach problems from genuinely different perspectives, while AI tools tend to converge.

### Key Mental Models
- **Complementary Feedback Sources**: AI provides consistent, broad evaluation; peers provide divergent perspectives — both are needed, and neither replaces the other
- **Defence as Understanding Test**: If you cannot explain a connection under questioning, you did not understand it when you drew it — the live format makes this visible
- **Preparation as Engagement**: Writing critique notes before the session forces genuine analysis of your partner's work, preventing shallow improvised feedback

### Critical Patterns
- Write preparation notes identifying specific gaps and challenges before the live session
- In the 15-minute session: identify 2+ missing connections, challenge 1+ feedback loop, defend your own map
- No AI access during the live session — test genuine understanding
- Write a 200-word post-session reflection addressing what you missed, what was hardest to defend, and what you would add

### Common Mistakes
- Treating the defence as a competition to "win" instead of an opportunity to discover blind spots
- Giving vague feedback like "your map is good" instead of identifying specific missing connections
- Thinking the live session replaces the AI Check — both are required because they test different capabilities
