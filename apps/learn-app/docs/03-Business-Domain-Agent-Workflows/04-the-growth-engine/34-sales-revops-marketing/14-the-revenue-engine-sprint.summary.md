# The Revenue Engine Sprint — Summary

## Core Concept

The capstone sprint assembles every component from Lessons 1-13 into a timed end-to-end execution: ICP validation, 5-prospect research-and-score sprint, Five Laws outreach, campaign brief with content calendar, and revenue dashboard — all in one session. No new concepts are introduced. The challenge is synthesis under time pressure and catching Agent Output Taxonomy errors during live execution, not in post-hoc review.

## Key Mental Models

- **Sprint as integration test** — individual components were built and tested in isolation; the sprint proves they connect into a coherent revenue engine where each stage's output feeds the next
- **Agent Output Taxonomy (five errors)** — Hallucinated Data (L01: unverifiable claims in research/campaign data), Miscalibrated Scoring (L03: ICP dimensions that penalise real buyers), Compliance Gap (L05: Five Laws or jurisdiction violations), Over-Automation (L06: sequences without exit conditions), Context Loss (L07: intelligence exists upstream but drops out downstream)
- **Revenue engine transferability** — the Five Laws, three-dimension scoring, pipeline stages, and dashboard structure are universal; ICP criteria, compliance jurisdictions, channel allocation, and content formats are business-specific reconfiguration
- **Minimum Viable Capstone** — Exercises 1, 2, 4, and 7 cover the core loop (validate ICP, sprint prospects, build campaign, produce dashboard); bonus exercises (3, 5, 6) deepen specific areas

## Critical Patterns

- ICP validation against closed-won deals must come first: if any closed-won deal scores below 60, the ICP has drifted and every subsequent score in the sprint will be unreliable
- Flag errors during execution, not after — mark Hallucinated Data and Verifiable claims inline without stopping the sprint to investigate
- The time comparison between first sprint (~45 min) and second sprint (~20-25 min) measures how much is setup vs execution; setup time drops to near-zero once config is dialled in
- The gap between demo data and real data is where you discover which components are robust (work despite messy inputs) and which are fragile (break when data is inconsistent)

## Common Mistakes

- Skipping ICP validation (Exercise 1) and going straight to prospecting — scoring five new prospects against a drifted ICP produces misleading rankings
- Stopping the sprint to verify every flagged claim — flag and continue; verification is post-sprint work
- Not auditing later sequence touches (Exercise 3) for personalisation decay — research intelligence gets consumed by early touches and is not replenished for later ones
- Accepting campaign budget allocations without verifying the underlying numbers (e.g., fabricated event costs that cascade into every downstream CPL projection)
- Treating the revenue engine as logistics-specific rather than recognising the universal structure: what transfers (scoring model, pipeline stages, Five Laws) vs what reconfigures (ICP content, compliance framework, data sources)

## Connections

- Integrates every lesson: L01-L04 (prospect intelligence), L05-L08 (sales execution), L09-L11 (content and campaigns), L12-L13 (compliance and operations)
- The division of labour principle that runs through every lesson is stated explicitly here: the agent researches, drafts, and recommends; the sales professional decides and sends
- Transferability analysis (Prompt 2) positions the revenue engine as a sellable product — configure it for a client's ICP, connect it to their CRM, tune thresholds, and the engine runs
- The capstone pattern (timed assembly of known components) mirrors the structure of other Part 4 capstone lessons across the book
