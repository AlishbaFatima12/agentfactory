# Pre-Call Briefs and Meeting Preparation; Summary

## Core Concept

A one-page pre-call brief containing five elements (quick context, call goal, tailored discovery questions, anticipated objections, success criteria) is the difference between a rep who wastes the first ten minutes confirming information already available and one who starts the conversation where the research left off. The lesson also surfaces Context Loss: when the agent produces generic output because prior research was not fed into the current prompt.

## Key Mental Models

- **Five brief elements**: Quick Context (who, what, relationship history), Call Goal (one sentence defining success), Discovery Questions (4-6 tailored to the prospect's specific situation), Anticipated Objections (2-3 likely pushbacks with prepared responses), Success Criteria (what "yes" looks like: a follow-up meeting, a pilot, a signed contract)
- **Context Loss** (agent error type #5): The agent operates without context that exists elsewhere in your workflow, producing generic output ("Tell me about your current workflow") instead of personalised intelligence. The agent does not remember across sessions: you manage the memory
- **Competitive battlecard integration**: A differentiation matrix (where we win, where they win, talk tracks, where we lose) is prepared before the call so competitive objections are anticipated, not improvised
- **Call type adaptation**: Discovery briefs ask qualifying questions. Demo briefs prepare for technical scrutiny. Follow-up briefs focus on removing specific blockers. The question set, objection handling, and success criteria all change by call type
- **Objection handling pattern**: Do not argue with timing objections ("too busy"): reframe the ask as small. Do not attack incumbent vendors: position your offer as vendor-neutral. Do not try to close on "not ready to buy": separate the assessment from the purchase decision
- **/call-summary for post-call processing**: Extracts internal summary, qualification status (BANT), action items with owners and deadlines, and a draft follow-up email from call notes or transcripts

## Critical Patterns

- Always feed prior research briefs into pre-call prompts: the five minutes it takes to paste prior research saves thirty minutes of generic discovery
- Evaluate discovery questions by asking: could this question be asked to a random company in the same industry? If yes, it is not tailored enough
- The diagnostic question for Context Loss: "Did the brief reference the research?" If no, you have detected the error: the fix is always feeding prior context
- Post-call follow-up should reference specific conversation points (e.g., "the HighJump to Manhattan Associates overlap") not generic language ("discuss how we can help")
- Action items split into [YOU] and [PROSPECT] with due dates: implicit commitments ("Let me look into that") are the ones that damage trust when forgotten

## Common Mistakes

- Winging discovery calls without preparation and spending the first ten minutes asking questions that could have been answered beforehand
- Building a pre-call brief without feeding prior research, resulting in generic questions indistinguishable from a cold call
- Using the same brief template for discovery, demo, and follow-up calls: each call type demands different question sets, objections, and success criteria
- Not verifying competitive battlecard claims (pricing, features, certifications) before using them in a meeting: competitive intelligence from the agent is a starting point, not a script
- Relying on the agent to remember prior sessions: it does not; context must be explicitly provided every time
