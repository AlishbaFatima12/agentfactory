# Campaign Performance Analysis — Summary

## Core Concept

Campaign analysis turns data into decisions, not dashboards. The base /performance-report produces channel-level observations; the extension adds ICP-filtered lead quality analysis, regional benchmark comparisons, and three-dimension scoring integration — revealing that the "problem" flagged by a global benchmark may actually be above-average performance locally. Every recommendation must pass three tests before action: is it specific enough to execute, is the expected impact realistic, and can the team actually do it this week.

## Key Mental Models

- **Observation vs actionable analysis** — "CTR is 0.72%" is observation; "CTR is above the Pakistan B2B benchmark of 0.65%; the real problem is that only 57% of downloads are ICP-matched" is actionable analysis
- **ICP-filtered analysis** — not all leads are equal; 28 downloads mean nothing if 43% come from non-ICP contacts; the real metric is cost per ICP-matched lead
- **Regional benchmarks vs global targets** — Pakistan LinkedIn CTR benchmarks are lower than global; UK LinkedIn CPL benchmarks are higher; applying one set of targets across markets produces misleading reports
- **Three-dimension scoring integration** — breaking HOT leads into Fit/Timing/Engagement reveals that leads may match the ICP and engage with content but lack active buying signals (timing weakness)
- **Monday-Wednesday-Friday cadence** — Monday = channel threshold check, Wednesday = sales-marketing alignment on lead quality, Friday = comprehensive /performance-report with 3 recommendations for next week

## Critical Patterns

- The extension's ICP-filtered analysis can reverse the base report's conclusions — what looks like a CTR problem may actually be a targeting problem
- Recommendations need a capacity check: suggesting 2 article pitches when the marketer is already at 3/week capacity means one must be deferred
- /competitive-brief produces positioning intelligence that directly feeds content calendar adjustments — competitive analysis is not a one-time exercise but a recurring input
- The weekly cadence creates a rhythm where Monday catches urgent threshold breaches, Wednesday aligns sales and marketing on quality, and Friday drives strategic adjustments

## Common Mistakes

- Acting on global benchmarks without checking regional norms — "fixing" LinkedIn CTR when it is already above the local benchmark
- Treating all leads equally in volume metrics instead of filtering by ICP match rate
- Accepting recommendations without testing for team capacity — a recommendation the team cannot execute this week is not actionable
- Running competitive analysis once instead of monthly — competitors adapt their positioning, and yesterday's gap may close
- Missing the distinction between the base report (what happened) and the extension analysis (what it means for your ICP)
