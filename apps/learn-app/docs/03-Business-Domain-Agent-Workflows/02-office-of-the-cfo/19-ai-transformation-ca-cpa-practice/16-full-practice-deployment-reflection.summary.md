### Core Concept

The final capstone deploys the entire AI-augmented CA/CPA practice as a single functioning system -- install all plugins, configure jurisdiction-specific global instructions, validate each domain workflow, schedule recurring tasks, stress-test with edge cases (currency conversion, multi-entity consolidation, cross-jurisdiction translation), document the deployment, and answer the chapter's defining question: "What is the work that only a qualified CA/CPA could do?"

### Key Mental Models

- **Integration Testing over Isolated Validation**: Single-domain practice labs test workflows in isolation; edge cases like multi-currency transactions and multi-entity consolidation force the system to use multiple plugins and extensions simultaneously, revealing integration failures that domain-specific testing would not catch.
- **Professional Value Proposition**: The question is not "what only a human could do" but "what specifically required my professional qualification, my judgment, and my liability" -- forming audit opinions, signing tax returns, advising on complex regulatory matters, and accepting engagement risk are the irreducible professional contributions.

### Critical Patterns

- The eight-step deployment sequence follows a quality-gated progression: install, configure, validate per domain, schedule recurring tasks, test cross-domain integration, document, stress-test edge cases, and reflect on professional boundaries.
- The capabilities statement for clients must be confident and professional rather than defensive -- honestly acknowledging what AI does while articulating the specific professional judgment the CA/CPA provides that AI cannot.
- The 90-day implementation plan sequences deployment by starting with highest-volume, lowest-judgment activities (Month 1), then parallel-running AI against manual processes for quality validation (Month 2), then communicating the change to clients with revised engagement letters and fee structures (Month 3).

### Common Mistakes

- Treating deployment as "turning everything on at once" rather than following the step-by-step validation and stress-testing sequence -- each step is a quality gate that catches problems before they reach client deliverables.
- Answering the defining question vaguely ("professional judgment") instead of specifically -- the strong answer identifies concrete decisions (materiality thresholds, going concern assessments, tax position defensibility, audit opinion formation) where professional qualification and liability create value that no AI system carries.
