### Core Concept

Four exercises span management accounting and GRC: a rolling 13-week cash flow forecast with stress testing, full board pack automation with cross-app orchestration, an enterprise risk register with COSO-aligned heat maps, and automated regulatory compliance monitoring with penalty-based risk stratification -- in each, the agent handles computation and formatting while the professional contributes scenario design, narrative interpretation, early warning identification, and risk prioritisation.

### Key Mental Models

- **Stress Scenario Design as Professional Judgment**: The agent can run any scenario through a cash flow model, but choosing which scenarios to test requires knowing the company's specific risks (customer concentration, supplier dependencies, seasonal patterns) -- generic worst-case scenarios miss the specific vulnerabilities that actually threaten the business.
- **Risk-Stratified Compliance Management**: A compliance calendar that only tracks deadlines treats all obligations equally; adding penalty severity, criminal liability provisions, and cumulative penalty calculations transforms deadline tracking into risk-informed prioritisation where high-consequence obligations receive proportionally more attention.

### Critical Patterns

- Management commentary in a board pack is interpretation, not summary -- the same set of numbers signals different things depending on business context, competitive dynamics, and strategic direction. The agent produces technically accurate summaries; the CFO-perspective narrative requires professional understanding.
- Enterprise risk registers are mechanical to build but the highest-value components are entity-specific early warning indicators (what would this particular company see in its data before a risk materialises) and the overall risk profile assessment.
- The receipts waterfall in cash flow forecasting tracks invoice cohorts through payment term buckets, making the forecast responsive to changes in collection patterns rather than using a single average days-outstanding assumption.

### Common Mistakes

- Treating stress testing as running worst-case numbers through the model -- the professional skill is choosing scenarios that test what actually keeps the CFO awake at night based on specific business risks, not generic pessimistic assumptions.
- Writing management commentary as a list of facts about what the numbers show rather than a narrative that connects financial results to business strategy, explains why variances matter (or do not), and frames the outlook in terms the board can act on.
