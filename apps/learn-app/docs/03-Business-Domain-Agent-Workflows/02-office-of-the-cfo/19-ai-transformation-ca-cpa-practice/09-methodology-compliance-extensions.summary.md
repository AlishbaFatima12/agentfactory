### Core Concept

Three additional extensions complete the five-extension architecture: audit methodology (encoding your firm's materiality, sampling, and documentation standards), client entity knowledge (encoding one client's business model, seasonal patterns, and risk areas), and regulatory compliance calendar (encoding filing deadlines, lead times, and penalties) -- together with jurisdiction and chart of accounts extensions, these five layers transform generic plugins into a practice-ready agent.

### Key Mental Models

- **Five-Extension Architecture**: Extensions 1, 3, and 5 apply broadly (jurisdiction-wide or firm-wide); Extensions 2 and 4 apply to individual entities -- this layered design ensures every output reflects jurisdiction rules, firm methodology, client context, and regulatory obligations simultaneously.
- **Method A Interview Framework**: Systematic extraction of tacit professional knowledge by asking three questions: what errors do juniors make (reveals rules to encode), what do you always check when reviewing (reveals quality validations), and what conditions cause you to escalate (reveals judgment boundaries).

### Critical Patterns

- Each significant client should have its own entity extension -- combining multiple clients into one extension risks applying one client's seasonal patterns or risk areas to another client's analysis.
- Compliance calendar extensions combined with Cowork scheduled tasks transform compliance from reactive calendar-checking into automated monitoring that alerts the CA/CPA when preparation needs to begin and escalates when deadlines are at risk.
- Method A answers convert directly to SKILL.md instructions: junior errors become standing rules, review questions become quality checks, escalation conditions become stop-and-flag triggers.

### Common Mistakes

- Building one massive extension covering all clients instead of one extension per significant client -- entity-specific knowledge varies between clients and should not cross-contaminate.
- Treating the compliance calendar as a simple deadline list instead of a risk management tool -- the real value is combining deadlines with penalty provisions and lead times to drive proactive preparation rather than reactive scrambling.
