### Core Concept

Three exercises cover the full spectrum of Domain 2 practice: corporate tax computation under Pakistan's ITO 2001 (compliance), M&A financial due diligence with DCF and comparable company analysis (transaction advisory), and restructuring scenario modelling with creditor waterfall analysis (restructuring advisory) -- in each, the agent handles computation while professional judgment determines which positions are defensible, which earnings adjustments are appropriate, and which restructuring scenario is achievable.

### Key Mental Models

- **Compliance vs Advisory Judgment**: Tax compliance follows deterministic rules (the agent computes correctly once jurisdiction rules are encoded), but identifying arguable positions that a tax authority might challenge is professional judgment no computation tool can make autonomously.
- **Normalisation as Professional Judgment**: In due diligence, the choice of which EBITDA items to adjust out (one-off costs, above-market owner compensation, pre-acquisition fees) directly determines the implied acquisition multiple -- these decisions serve the interests of buyer or seller depending on which adjustments are included.

### Critical Patterns

- The accounting-to-taxable-income bridge with statutory add-backs and deductions follows a universal structure across jurisdictions -- specific provisions differ but the workflow pattern (accounting profit, adjustments, tax liability, credits, net payable) transfers directly.
- The minimum tax check (Section 113 in Pakistan, equivalent provisions elsewhere) catches cases where regular tax understates the actual liability -- a step that is easy to skip but carries real financial consequences.
- In restructuring, running three parallel scenarios (consensual, formal arrangement, asset sale) is what makes the recommendation informed -- the professional value is in judging which scenario is achievable given creditor dynamics and management credibility, not in the financial modelling itself.

### Common Mistakes

- Taking DCF or comparable company valuation output at face value without questioning the input assumptions -- growth rate, WACC, terminal value, and comparable selection are where professional judgment determines whether the valuation is meaningful or misleading.
- Treating restructuring modelling as producing a "right answer" -- the three scenarios provide quantitative foundation, but the recommendation requires understanding relationships between stakeholders that no financial model captures.
