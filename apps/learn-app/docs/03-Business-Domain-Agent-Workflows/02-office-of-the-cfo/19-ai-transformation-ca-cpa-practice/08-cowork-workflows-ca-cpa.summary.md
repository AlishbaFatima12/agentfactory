### Core Concept

A complete month-end close can be orchestrated as a Cowork workflow -- scheduled reconciliations run at 7 AM, the CA/CPA reviews exceptions in 30 minutes instead of building reconciliations over 3 hours, and the remaining workflow (journal entries, management accounts, variance analysis, board presentation) completes by midday -- shifting the professional's time from execution to judgment.

### Key Mental Models

- **Execution-to-Judgment Shift**: The value change is not hours saved but how those hours are spent -- before Cowork, 7.5 hours of process execution; after, 1 hour 45 minutes of reviewing outputs and writing management commentary where business knowledge is irreplaceable.
- **Global Instructions as Persistent Context**: Setting jurisdiction, currency, reporting standards, materiality thresholds, and variance comparisons once in Cowork global instructions ensures every workflow reflects your practice context without repeating it each session.

### Critical Patterns

- Cross-app orchestration (Excel to PowerPoint) eliminates the manual handoff that is both the most time-consuming and most error-prone step in board pack preparation.
- The professional judgment boundary is consistent across all workflows: the agent handles data assembly, mechanical computation, formatting, and scheduling; the CA/CPA handles exception assessment, classification judgment, business context interpretation, and sign-off authority.
- Global instructions should establish broad practice context (jurisdiction, standards, currency, materiality) rather than narrow task-specific instructions -- the same global context applies across different workflows.

### Common Mistakes

- Writing global instructions that are too narrow or task-specific -- effective instructions establish the practice environment broadly enough to apply across reconciliations, financial statements, variance analysis, and board packs.
- Confusing time compression with role elimination -- the month-end close still requires a CA/CPA; the role shifts from executing reconciliations to reviewing exceptions and writing management commentary.
