### Core Concept

A 20-jurisdiction reference map classifies every major Islamic finance market into one of three accounting regimes, and a four-jurisdiction murabaha comparison proves that the compliance difference is entirely in labels and classification: never in the arithmetic.

### Key Mental Models

- **The Global Standards Map as Lookup Tool**: Rather than memorizing 20 jurisdictions, the map is a reference table an agent checks before every query: identifying primary standard, AAOIFI role, regulator, and unique features for each jurisdiction.
- **Compliance vs Calculation Distinction**: The effective profit rate method produces identical numbers everywhere; the compliance layer is entirely about which labels, balance sheet categories, and disclosure structures the jurisdiction requires.

### Critical Patterns

- Regime 1 (AAOIFI Primary): Bahrain, Qatar, Sudan: financial statements use Shariah-characterised terminology like "Murabaha Receivables" and "Equity of Investment Account Holders"
- Regime 2 (IFRS with Islamic guidance): Malaysia, UAE, Saudi Arabia, UK, Turkey: IFRS governs measurement while Islamic disclosures are supplemental
- Regime 3 (Local Standards): Iran, Bangladesh: local standard-setters blend Islamic jurisprudence with local requirements, differing materially from both AAOIFI and IFRS
- Pakistan straddles Regimes 1 and 2, requiring IFRS measurement with AAOIFI-informed terminology and SBP-specific disclosures

### Common Mistakes

- Defaulting to IFRS for all Islamic finance work: AAOIFI-primary jurisdictions like Bahrain require fundamentally different financial statement presentation
- Confusing "cosmetic" label differences with compliance-critical ones: using "Loans and Advances" in Bahrain is a regulatory violation, not a style preference
