### Core Concept

UK Islamic banking operates under identical PRA/FCA regulation as conventional banks with no Islamic exemptions, while HMRC's Finance Act 2005 tax equivalence ensures Islamic products are taxed identically to conventional counterparts: creating a deliberate divergence where accounting labels avoid "interest" but tax characterisation treats payments as interest-equivalent.

### Key Mental Models

- **Triple Characterisation**: Diminishing musharaka home finance is simultaneously co-ownership (Shariah), a financial asset at amortised cost (IFRS 9 substance-over-form), and a mortgage-equivalent (HMRC tax): each framework asks a different question about the same product, and all three characterisations are correct within their own domain.
- **Tax Equivalence, Not Tax Advantage**: HMRC's legislation ensures Islamic products are neither advantaged nor disadvantaged compared to conventional counterparts: DM rental is taxed as interest, SDLT relief prevents double stamp duty, and sukuk distributions get interest-equivalent treatment.

### Critical Patterns

- IFRS 9 substance-over-form: DM passes the business model test (held-to-collect) and SPPI test (cash flows represent principal plus return), classifying as amortised cost; the effective interest rate method produces front-loaded income
- SDLT relief (Finance Act 2003 sections 71A-73) prevents double taxation on Islamic mortgages: stamp duty charged on one transfer only
- PRA applies identical Basel III capital requirements: same risk weights for DM and conventional mortgage exposures, same regulatory returns, no IFSB standards adoption
- Income labels: "Profit from Home Finance" or "Income from Islamic Financing": never "Interest Income" or "Mortgage Interest"

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming UK Islamic banks have separate or lighter regulatory treatment: the PRA grants no Islamic-specific concessions; Basel III applies identically
- Thinking HMRC tax equivalence means Islamic products are taxed differently: it means the opposite: they are taxed identically to prevent disadvantage
