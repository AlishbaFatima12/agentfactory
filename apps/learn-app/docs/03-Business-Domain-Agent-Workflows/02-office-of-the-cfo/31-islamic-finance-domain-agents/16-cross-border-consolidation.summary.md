### Core Concept

When an AAOIFI parent (Bahrain) consolidates IFRS subsidiaries (UAE, Malaysia, Pakistan), three problems arise that have no conventional equivalent: IAH fund classification (separate category vs financial liability), intra-group murabaha (genuine trading transaction vs funding arrangement), and cross-jurisdiction Shariah compliance gaps: the pragmatic industry answer is IFRS primary for the consolidated group with AAOIFI supplementary disclosures.

### Key Mental Models

- **IAH Reclassification Is Conceptual, Not Presentational**: Under AAOIFI, IAH depositors theoretically bear investment risk (funds are not guaranteed). Under IFRS, practical regulatory and competitive reality means banks do not let depositors suffer losses, making the funds a financial liability. The group must choose one view for consolidated statements: this is a substance-over-form judgment, not a label change.
- **Intra-Group Murabaha as Shariah Escalation Point**: A $200M murabaha from a Bahrain parent to a UAE subsidiary may involve an actual asset purchase or may be merely an inter-company funding arrangement with an Islamic label. Whether both group entities knowingly using a "purchase" as a funding mechanism satisfies the real-asset-backing requirement is a question only the SSB can resolve.

### Critical Patterns

- Five consolidation adjustment categories distinguish Islamic from conventional group reporting: IAH fund reclassification, ijarah asset treatment, income label harmonisation, impairment methodology alignment, and financial instrument classification criteria
- The group framework recommendation is IFRS primary because the majority of subsidiaries and international investors require IFRS: the Bahrain parent adds AAOIFI supplementary disclosures for local regulatory compliance
- Transfer pricing for intra-group mudarib fees must satisfy both Shariah compliance (arm's-length from an Islamic perspective) and Pakistan FBR tax authority requirements (transfer pricing documentation, withholding tax under the Pakistan-Bahrain treaty)
- Intra-group murabaha elimination follows standard consolidation principles, but the Shariah compliance question about whether one Islamic bank can structure a murabaha with a group entity is genuinely open

### Common Mistakes

- Treating consolidation adjustments as purely mechanical: the IAH reclassification involves a conceptual decision about the nature of the instrument, not just relabelling
- Assuming intra-group murabaha is identical to a conventional inter-company loan for elimination purposes: the Shariah compliance question about real economic substance must be escalated to the SSB
