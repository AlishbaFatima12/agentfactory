### Core Concept

Takaful is mutual insurance where participants collectively bear insurance risk (not the operator), creating a fundamental IFRS 17 question (who is the insurer?) that different jurisdictions answer differently, with Malaysia's BNM providing the most detailed guidance globally.

### Key Mental Models

- **Who Bears the Risk?**: In conventional insurance, the insurer bears risk. In takaful, participants collectively bear risk through a common fund; the operator merely manages for a fee. This changes who holds IFRS 17 insurance contracts.
- **Dual Financial Statements**: Every takaful entity maintains two sets of accounts (the operator's statements (wakala fee revenue, management expenses) and the Participants' Fund statement (contributions minus fees minus claims plus investment income)) with different economic drivers for each.

### Critical Patterns

- Three operating models: wakala (fixed management fee), mudaraba (investment income sharing), hybrid (most common in practice: wakala for underwriting, mudaraba for investment)
- Qard hasan is the most analytically demanding issue: when the Participants' Fund enters deficit, the operator provides an interest-free loan, creating an IAS 37 question about whether to recognise a provision or disclose a contingent liability
- PAA applies to general takaful (motor, property, medical with coverage under 12 months); GMM applies to family takaful (long-term life-equivalent contracts)
- Malaysia's BNM rules that IFRS 17 applies to the operator; UAE and UK have no equivalent ruling, requiring professional judgment

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming the takaful operator bears insurance risk: the operator earns fees; participants bear risk collectively through the fund
- Booking qard hasan as the operator's expense immediately: it is an interest-free loan (receivable on the operator's books) that may later be impaired if the fund cannot repay
