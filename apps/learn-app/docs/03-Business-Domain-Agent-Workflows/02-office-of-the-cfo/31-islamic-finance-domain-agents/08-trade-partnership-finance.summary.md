### Core Concept

Islamic finance has two remaining product families beyond murabaha, ijarah, sukuk, and takaful: **trade finance** (salam and istisna'a) funds tangible assets, while **partnership finance** (mudaraba and musharaka) shares venture profit and loss. The AAOIFI-vs-IFRS divergence is sharpest in musharaka, where profit-share returns fail the IFRS 9 SPPI test and force Fair Value Through Profit or Loss classification: a fundamentally different measurement basis from AAOIFI historical cost.

### Key Mental Models

- **Trade vs Partnership pairs**: Salam and istisna'a fund the purchase or construction of a specific asset; mudaraba and musharaka provide capital and share in venture outcomes. The accounting mechanics differ because the risk profile differs: commodity price risk versus venture performance risk.
- **SPPI test as framework boundary**: IFRS 9 assumes debt instruments pay principal plus interest. Musharaka returns depend on venture performance, not a contractual rate, so they fail SPPI and must be marked to market. This is where the two frameworks diverge most.
- **Loss sharing is non-negotiable**: Profit ratios can be negotiated freely, but losses must be shared strictly by capital contribution ratio: a Shariah rule with no exception.

### Critical Patterns

- Salam requires full advance payment at contract date; three journal entries cover payment, delivery, and resale
- Istisna'a uses percentage of completion revenue recognition with milestone-based Shariah compliance confirmations alongside financial measurement
- Mudaraba profit distribution follows a six-step waterfall: total income, bank/IAH allocation, mudarib share, weightage tiers, PER deduction, IRR deduction
- Musharaka losses reduce the carrying value of each partner's investment proportionally: sustained losses write the investment to zero

### Common Mistakes

- Applying the negotiated profit ratio to losses instead of the mandatory capital ratio
- Treating parallel salam or parallel istisna'a as linked contracts: they must be legally independent or the structure is Shariah non-compliant
- Expecting musharaka investments to pass the SPPI test under IFRS 9: profit shares are not interest, so FVTPL classification is the typical outcome
