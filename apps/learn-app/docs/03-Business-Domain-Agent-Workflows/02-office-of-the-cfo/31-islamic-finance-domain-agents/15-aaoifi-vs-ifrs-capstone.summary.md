### Core Concept

Building complete financial statements under both AAOIFI and IFRS for the same Islamic bank reveals that the five material differences between the frameworks (IAH classification, ijarah asset treatment, income labels, impairment methodology, and zakat treatment) produce different balance sheet structures and ratios from identical underlying economics, with the IAH classification being the single most material divergence.

### Key Mental Models

- **IAH Classification as the Dominant Difference**: Investment Account Holder funds (65% of a typical Bahraini Islamic bank's funding) appear as a separate balance sheet category under AAOIFI (neither liability nor equity) but as financial liabilities under IFRS (IAS 32). This single reclassification changes the reported leverage ratio, equity base, and all equity-denominated ratios.
- **ROE Divergence as Accounting Artefact**: AAOIFI's larger apparent equity base (because IAH are not classified as liabilities) produces a lower ROE; IFRS's smaller equity base (because IAH are liabilities) produces a higher ROE. The actual profitability is identical: the multi-percentage-point ROE difference is entirely a measurement artefact of framework choice.

### Critical Patterns

- Five material AAOIFI/IFRS differences drive the reconciliation: (1) IAH classification: separate category vs financial liability, (2) ijarah assets: on balance sheet at depreciated cost under AAOIFI FAS 32 vs net investment in lease under IFRS 16, (3) income labels: product-specific ("Murabaha Income") vs generic ("Financing Income"), (4) impairment: FAS 30 staging vs IFRS 9 ECL staging, (5) zakat treatment: disclosed in Shariah report vs possible P&L expense
- Income measurement is arithmetically identical in most cases; the differences are in presentation, classification, and balance sheet structure; not in the numbers themselves
- Framework-tension audit risks include dual-reporting inconsistency, label compliance violations, ijarah asset misclassification, IAH substance-over-form questions, and impairment methodology divergence
- An investor comparing a Bahrain AAOIFI bank with a UAE IFRS bank must adjust for framework differences or risk systematically undervaluing AAOIFI-reporting banks

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming AAOIFI and IFRS produce materially different income numbers: the income measurement is usually arithmetically identical; the differences are in presentation and balance sheet structure
- Interpreting the ROE difference between AAOIFI and IFRS as a performance difference: it is entirely caused by the equity denominator size, not by any difference in profitability
