### Core Concept

The same bank produces different zakat obligations depending on jurisdiction because the ZATCA equity-based formula (Saudi Arabia) and the Hanafi liquid-assets formula (most other jurisdictions) start from opposite sides of the balance sheet — practitioners must know which formula governs before calculating.

### Key Mental Models

- **Equity-Based vs Assets-Based Starting Points**: ZATCA starts from shareholders' equity minus long-term assets; Hanafi/AAOIFI GS9 starts from liquid zakatable assets minus current liabilities. For a bank with large long-term investments, the two formulas can produce materially different numbers from identical data.
- **Four Treatment Categories**: Saudi Arabia (mandatory P&L expense replacing income tax), Pakistan (mandatory deduction-at-source as agent — never the bank's own expense), Bahrain (voluntary institutional zakat with annual report disclosure), Malaysia (voluntary with Hanafi or Shafi'i methodology).

### Critical Patterns

- Pakistan's mechanism is structurally unique: banks deduct 2.5% from qualifying deposit accounts on the first day of Ramadan as agent for the Central Zakat Administration — this is a pass-through that never touches the bank's income statement
- Non-Shariah income purification is separate from zakat: inadvertent interest or non-halal dividends must be donated to charity and cannot offset zakat obligations
- The nisab threshold (minimum wealth level) applies universally but is clearly exceeded for institutional calculations
- ZATCA zakat replaces income tax for Saudi-owned equity; foreign-owned portions pay 20% income tax instead — mixed ownership requires proportionate calculation

### Common Mistakes

- Applying the Hanafi formula to a Saudi bank — ZATCA has its own mandatory equity-based formula that differs materially from AAOIFI GS9
- Booking Pakistan's depositor zakat deductions as the bank's own expense — the bank is an agent; the entry is a liability-to-liability pass-through
