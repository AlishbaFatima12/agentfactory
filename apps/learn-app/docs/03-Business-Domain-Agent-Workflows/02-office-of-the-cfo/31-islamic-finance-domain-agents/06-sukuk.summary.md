### Core Concept

Sukuk are certificates of ownership in underlying assets (not bonds), and the IFRS 9 SPPI test determines whether an investor classifies them at amortised cost or fair value: while the issuer's purchase undertaking almost always prevents asset derecognition, making most sukuk financial liabilities under IAS 32.

### Key Mental Models

- **The SPPI Test as Classification Gateway**: Ijarah sukuk (fixed rental distributions, purchase undertaking at face value) typically pass the SPPI test and qualify for amortised cost; musharakah and mudarabah sukuk (variable profit-linked returns) fail SPPI and must be measured at FVTPL: this classification drives whether investors report stable or volatile carrying values.
- **Issuer Derecognition Logic**: A purchase undertaking at face value means the issuer retains substantially all risks and rewards of the underlying assets, so derecognition fails under IFRS 9: assets stay on the issuer's balance sheet and sukuk proceeds become a financial liability.

### Critical Patterns

- The same sukuk held by investors in three jurisdictions gets classified under three frameworks: AAOIFI FAS 25 (Bahrain), MFRS 9 (Malaysia), IFRS 9 (UK): same instrument, different classification systems and income labels
- AAOIFI Draft Standard 62 could shift sukuk from asset-based to asset-backed, requiring genuine asset transfer and potentially restructuring most existing sukuk globally
- The purchase undertaking is the most contested issue: it makes sukuk economically similar to bonds while Shariah Standard 59 permits it for ijarah but prohibits it for equity-based sukuk
- Green sukuk combine Islamic finance's asset-backing requirements with ESG principles, representing a natural convergence and the fastest-growing market segment

### Common Mistakes

- Treating sukuk as bonds: sukuk represent ownership of assets with rental returns, not debt obligations with interest payments
- Assuming the SPPI test result is the same for all sukuk types: equity-linked structures (musharakah, mudarabah) fail SPPI while debt-like structures (ijarah) pass
