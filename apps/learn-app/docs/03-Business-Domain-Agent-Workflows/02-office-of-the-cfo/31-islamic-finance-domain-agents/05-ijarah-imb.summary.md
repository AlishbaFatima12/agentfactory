### Core Concept

Ijarah (Islamic lease) produces the most consequential divergence between AAOIFI and IFRS: under AAOIFI FAS 32, the leased asset stays on the lessor's balance sheet throughout, while under IFRS 16, an IMB is typically classified as a finance lease and the asset is derecognised, fundamentally changing balance sheet structure and regulatory capital ratios.

### Key Mental Models

- **Usufruct vs Ownership**: The lessee pays for the right to use the asset (usufruct), not for the asset itself: the lessor retains ownership throughout under Shariah principles, which is why AAOIFI keeps the asset on the lessor's books.
- **Substance vs Form Divergence**: AAOIFI follows the Shariah form (lessor owns, lessor reports the asset), while IFRS 16 follows economic substance (risks and rewards may have transferred to the lessee, so the asset is derecognised from the lessor).

### Critical Patterns

- AAOIFI FAS 32 depreciates over the asset's useful life (not the lease term), creating a deliberate book loss when ownership transfers at IMB end: this is the correct Shariah outcome, not an error
- IFRS 16 finance lease: lessor derecognises the asset and recognises a net investment in the lease (a receivable replaces the physical asset)
- IFRS 16 lessee: recognises a right-of-use asset and lease liability regardless of the Islamic structure
- For a bank with billions in IMB assets, the framework choice determines whether the balance sheet shows physical assets or financial receivables: affecting regulatory capital calculations and lending capacity
- The IMB ownership transfer promise must be a separate document from the lease contract under Shariah requirements

### Common Mistakes

- Depreciating IMB assets over the lease term under AAOIFI: the standard requires depreciation over the asset's useful life, even when that exceeds the lease term
- Assuming the Islamic structure exempts the lessee from IFRS 16: corporate lessees in IFRS jurisdictions must apply IFRS 16 regardless of Shariah form
