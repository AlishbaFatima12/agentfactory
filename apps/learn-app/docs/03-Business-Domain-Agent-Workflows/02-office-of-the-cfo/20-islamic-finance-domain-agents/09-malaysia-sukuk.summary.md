### Core Concept

Malaysia is the world's largest single sukuk market (35-40% of global issuance), applying MFRS (IFRS-equivalent) — not AAOIFI — which means sukuk musharakah classification follows IAS 32 substance-over-form analysis, and the green SRI Sukuk Framework adds disclosure obligations without changing accounting measurement.

### Key Mental Models

- **Shariah Form vs Accounting Substance**: Most Malaysian sukuk musharakah have fixed distributions and purchase undertakings at face value, making them financial liabilities under IAS 32 despite the partnership (musharakah) label — the distinction between Shariah legal structure and accounting classification is the core analytical skill.
- **Green = Disclosure Overlay, Not Measurement Change**: The SRI Sukuk Framework requires use-of-proceeds tracking and impact reporting but does not alter MFRS 9 recognition, measurement, or IAS 32 classification — the numbers are identical to non-green sukuk.

### Critical Patterns

- Three institutions govern: SC Malaysia (capital markets, SRI framework), BNM (banking, SAC rulings), MASB (accounting standards — MFRS applies, no separate Islamic standards)
- Issuer: sukuk with fixed distributions and redemption at face value = financial liability under IAS 32; distributions are finance cost in the income statement, not equity distributions
- Investor: EPF (largest retirement fund) sets the benchmark; ijarah and fixed-distribution musharakah sukuk pass the SPPI test for amortised cost classification
- Income labels: "Finance Cost — Sukuk Distribution" or "Profit Paid on Sukuk" for issuers; never "Interest Expense"

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming Malaysia uses AAOIFI for accounting — it uses MFRS (IFRS-equivalent) with MASB Islamic application guidance; AAOIFI is only voluntary Shariah reference
- Thinking green sukuk has different accounting numbers — the green designation changes disclosures and reporting obligations only, not recognition or measurement
