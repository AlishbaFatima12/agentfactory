### Core Concept

Saudi Arabia applies IFRS (not AAOIFI) for financial reporting, with Al Rajhi Bank as the global IFRS benchmark for Islamic banking — but the most distinctive feature is the ZATCA equity-based zakat formula, which starts from shareholders' equity and replaces income tax for Saudi-owned companies.

### Key Mental Models

- **ZATCA Equity-Based Zakat**: The formula (share capital + reserves + retained earnings minus fixed assets minus long-term investments) times 2.5% is fundamentally different from the Hanafi liquid-assets formula used elsewhere — for a bank with large long-term investments, the two formulas produce materially different obligations from identical balance sheet data.
- **Al Rajhi as Benchmark**: Al Rajhi Bank's IFRS presentation (murabaha receivables as financing assets, "Income from Murabaha and Instalment Sales" caption, IFRS 9 three-stage ECL) sets the standard that auditors and regulators expect from all Saudi IFIs.

### Critical Patterns

- Saudi IFIs use "Murabaha Income" by convention even under IFRS — acceptable because IFRS requires disclosure of income nature, not a specific label; "Interest Income" is never used
- ZATCA zakat replaces income tax for Saudi-owned equity; foreign-owned equity portions pay 20% income tax instead — mixed ownership requires proportionate calculation
- PIF sukuk for Vision 2030 projects require IFRS 9 classification; ijarah sukuk with fixed distributions typically pass SPPI for amortised cost or FVOCI
- FVOCI classification exposes the bank to OCI volatility from yield movements without income statement impact

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming Saudi Arabia uses AAOIFI for accounting — AAOIFI is Shariah compliance guidance only; IFRS governs all financial reporting
- Confusing ZATCA zakat with religious Hanafi zakat calculation — ZATCA's formula is administratively determined and starts from equity, not from liquid assets
