### Core Concept

Four competing Shariah screening methodologies (SC Malaysia, Tadawul, AAOIFI Standard 21, MSCI Islamic) agree on core sector prohibitions but diverge on financial ratio thresholds and denominators — the same company can be Shariah-compliant under one methodology and non-compliant under another, making the fund's documented methodology choice a material compliance decision.

### Key Mental Models

- **Denominator Divergence**: SC Malaysia and Tadawul use total assets (stable balance sheet figure) while MSCI and AAOIFI use market capitalisation (volatile market figure). A company with unchanged debt can breach MSCI thresholds during a share price decline while remaining compliant under SC Malaysia — the debt did not change, only the denominator.
- **Hard vs Soft Exclusions**: Hard exclusions (conventional banking, alcohol, gambling, pork, weapons of mass destruction) are zero-tolerance across all four methodologies. Soft exclusions (defence, hotels, mixed entertainment) proceed to the Non-Permissible Income test at the 5% threshold.

### Critical Patterns

- The NPI 5% threshold is universal across all four methodologies: if non-permissible revenue exceeds 5% of total revenue, the company is excluded; if it passes but NPI is above zero, a purification obligation arises on dividends received
- Purification is calculated as each holding's dividend multiplied by that company's NPI percentage, then donated to charity — it cannot be retained or offset against income
- Three divergence scenarios recur professionally: denominator divergence (total assets vs market cap), sector classification divergence (different methodology definitions), and NPI definition divergence (narrow vs broad interpretation of non-permissible services)
- The fund's SSB must have a documented resolution policy — either the conservative approach (exclude if any methodology excludes) or the selective approach (apply the methodology specified in the SSB-approved investment policy)

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming all four methodologies produce identical results — the denominator difference alone can flip a company's compliance status during market downturns without any change in the company's actual debt
- Treating purification as optional — any holding that passes with NPI above zero carries a mandatory purification obligation on dividends received
