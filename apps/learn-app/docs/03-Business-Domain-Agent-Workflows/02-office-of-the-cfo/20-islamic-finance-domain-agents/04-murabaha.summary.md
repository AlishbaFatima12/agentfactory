### Core Concept

Murabaha is a sale transaction (not a loan) with a universal four-step journal entry sequence — the arithmetic is identical under AAOIFI FAS 2 and IFRS 9, but the income labels, receivable classifications, and disclosure structures differ by jurisdiction.

### Key Mental Models

- **Four-Step Journal Entry Sequence**: (1) Bank purchases asset, (2) Bank sells to customer at disclosed mark-up creating deferred income, (3) Periodic profit recognition via effective profit rate, (4) Customer instalment payments reduce the receivable — this sequence is universal across all jurisdictions.
- **Effective Profit Rate = Effective Interest Rate**: The calculation is arithmetically identical to IFRS 9's effective interest rate method; only the label differs ("effective profit rate" vs "effective interest rate").

### Critical Patterns

- Four Shariah conditions must be confirmed before any accounting entry: actual purchase by the bank, disclosed mark-up, binding offer and acceptance, specified payment terms
- AAOIFI labels: "Murabaha Income" and "Murabaha Receivables" (never under "Loans and Advances")
- IFRS labels: "Profit from Islamic Financing" with receivables under or alongside "Loans and Advances"
- Commodity murabaha (tawarruq) has identical accounting but different Shariah scrutiny — organised tawarruq is controversial because the commodity serves no economic purpose
- No additional charges on overdue amounts — this prohibition of penalty interest fundamentally changes credit risk economics versus conventional banking

### Common Mistakes

- Treating murabaha as a loan with interest — it is a sale with disclosed mark-up, and the terminology must reflect this in every jurisdiction
- Omitting Shariah structural verification before generating entries — if the bank did not actually purchase the asset before reselling, the contract may be void
