### Core Concept

Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) advisory is the CA/CPA domain most resilient to AI displacement because it requires synthesizing regulatory interpretation, organizational context, and stakeholder judgment -- but AI transforms GRC from periodic manual testing to continuous automated monitoring with real-time exception escalation.

### Key Mental Models

- **Three Lines Model**: Line 1 (management owns and manages risk), Line 2 (oversight functions monitor and challenge), Line 3 (independent assurance provides objective evaluation) -- AI enhances each line differently: automating Line 1 controls, enabling continuous Line 2 monitoring, and expanding Line 3 coverage from samples to populations.
- **Continuous vs Periodic Controls Testing**: Traditional GRC tests controls periodically (quarterly, annually); AI-powered monitoring tests continuously, catching control failures in real-time rather than discovering them months later during an audit.

### Critical Patterns

- Enterprise risk registers become living documents when AI agents continuously scan for emerging risks, update likelihood/impact scores, and flag threshold breaches rather than relying on quarterly manual reviews.
- Regulatory compliance calendars automated with AI monitoring track filing deadlines across multiple regulators and jurisdictions, preventing the common failure of missed deadlines due to manual tracking.
- GRC advisory judgment -- interpreting what a regulation means for a specific organization's context -- remains the highest-value, least automatable skill in the entire CA/CPA profession.

### Common Mistakes

- Implementing continuous monitoring tools without redesigning the escalation and response workflows -- automated detection without automated triage creates alert fatigue that is worse than periodic manual review.
- Treating all three lines of the governance model as equally automatable -- Line 1 controls execution automates well, but Line 2 and Line 3 judgment functions require significant human oversight.
