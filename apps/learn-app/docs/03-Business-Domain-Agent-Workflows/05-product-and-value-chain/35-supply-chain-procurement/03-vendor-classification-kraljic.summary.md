### Core Concept

Classification must happen before assessment: the Kraljic tier determines how deep you go, how often you review, and what actions you take. Supply risk (can you replace this vendor?) beats spend level as the critical axis.

### Key Mental Models

- **Two Axes, Four Quadrants**: Supply risk × profit impact produces Strategic (high/high), Tactical (low/high), Commodity (low/low), and Bottleneck (high/low): each with its own management model
- **Bottleneck Trap**: Low-spend vendors with no alternatives are classified Bottleneck and require the same quarterly review frequency as Strategic vendors: their spend is low but their failure risk is maximum
- **Classification Is Dynamic**: A Tactical vendor becomes Strategic if competitors exit the market; a Bottleneck becomes Commodity when you qualify an alternative: review classifications at least annually

### Critical Patterns

- Always classify before assessing: tier determines assessment depth, review frequency, and risk thresholds in every downstream workflow
- Ask "what happens if this vendor fails tomorrow?" rather than "how much do we spend?" to determine supply risk
- Build and maintain a classification register; this register feeds Lessons 4, 7, and 13 directly
- When `/vendor-assess` output disagrees with your manual classification, trace the rationale: one of you is applying the wrong criteria

### Common Mistakes

- Equating high spend with Strategic classification: a high-spend vendor with many alternatives is Tactical, not Strategic
- Treating low spend as low risk: a sole-source supplier of a production-critical component is the highest-risk vendor regardless of contract value
- Treating classification as a one-time exercise: markets consolidate, suppliers exit, qualification programs succeed
