### Core Concept

Spend analytics answers three questions that individual purchase records cannot: who are you buying from (consolidation opportunity), what are you paying (price consistency), and what should you be paying (market benchmark). The overpayment pattern: multiple sites buying identical specifications at different prices with no group visibility: is normal, not exceptional.

### Key Mental Models

- **Three Questions, Three Analysis Types**: Consolidation analysis (volume opportunity) → Price consistency check (same item, different prices across sites) → Market benchmark (your price vs. market). Each builds on the previous.
- **Savings Pipeline Stages**: Identified → Active → Captured. An identified saving is not delivered until the contract is signed: pipeline discipline prevents premature reporting.
- **Continuous vs. Periodic**: The `spend-intelligence-agent` flags new price inconsistencies as they emerge; the analysis cycle captures the backlog. Both are required.

### Critical Patterns

- Run price consistency checks across all sites before market benchmarking: internal overpayment is often larger than the gap to market
- Require cited source and quality rating on every market benchmark output: benchmark data quality varies significantly; low-quality benchmarks mislead negotiation strategy
- Structure the savings business case around captured savings, not identified; CPOs are evaluated on delivered results
- Use PO compliance rate as a leading indicator of spend visibility: low PO compliance means the spend data is incomplete before any analysis

### Common Mistakes

- Treating spend analytics as a reporting function: the output should be a negotiation mandate, not a dashboard
- Reporting identified savings as achieved: until the contract is signed and the price changes, the saving is a target
- Running consolidation analysis without checking PO compliance first: analysing incomplete spend data produces unreliable consolidation candidates
