### Core Concept

Every supply chain problem is an information problem first: the data to detect reconciliation errors, vendor distress, and outdated routing decisions already exists in organisational systems — the failure is in connecting and attending to it at the right time.

### Key Mental Models

- **Physical-Digital Gap**: Supply chains live in two worlds simultaneously — physical reality and digital records. Costs hide in the gap between them.
- **Data Without Connection**: All three structural failures occur despite abundant data. The problem is integration and attention, not collection.
- **Annual Review vs. Continuous Signal**: Events like supplier financial stress develop over months of visible signals; annual reviews only catch crises after they have occurred.

### Critical Patterns

- Recognise the Reconciliation Swamp by its economics: 15-25% exception rates at £25-£80 per invoice in staff time to resolve
- Identify the Vendor Blind Spot when monitoring fails not from lack of data but from inability to connect signals across systems simultaneously
- Spot the Static Optimisation Trap when network decisions are running on assumptions more than 18 months old
- Map any supply chain symptom (late payment, disruption, cost overrun) back to its specific information gap

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming the organisation "doesn't have enough data" — all three failures occur with abundant data; the problem is integration
- Treating annual supplier reviews as sufficient monitoring — financial stress and delivery decline develop over weeks to months
- Believing an ERP handles reconciliation — ERPs flag exceptions but do not resolve them; resolution requires human judgment
