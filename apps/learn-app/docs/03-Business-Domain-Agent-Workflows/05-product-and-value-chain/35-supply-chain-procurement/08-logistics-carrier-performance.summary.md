### Core Concept

Logistics costs accumulate quietly between contract reviews — carrier performance shifts and lane conditions change, but routing decisions made at contract time keep running unchanged. The `/logistics-brief` skill makes this a continuous analytical capability rather than a periodic exercise.

### Key Mental Models

- **Total Cost vs. Rate Per Kg**: A cheap carrier can be a net cost generator once downstream failure costs (redelivery, customer service, damage claims) are included — cost per kg is an incomplete decision criterion
- **Expedited Freight as Upstream Signal**: If premium freight exceeds 10% of logistics spend, the root cause is upstream (late supplier delivery, forecast error, inventory positioning) — not a logistics shortfall. Buying more premium capacity solves nothing.
- **Four-Metric Scorecard**: OTD + damage rate + cost per kg + track-and-trace reliability combine into a composite that classifies carriers as Outperforming, Compliant, Underperforming, or Review-required

### Critical Patterns

- Use total cost analysis, not rate per kg, when evaluating carrier performance — include downstream failure costs
- When expedited freight exceeds 10% of logistics spend, investigate upstream root causes before renegotiating carrier contracts
- Run `/logistics-brief` on the full lane portfolio, not just problem lanes — quiet lanes accumulate the largest hidden savings
- Own fleet justification is delivery type-specific: critical deliveries may warrant the premium; standard B2B routes typically do not

### Common Mistakes

- Selecting carriers on cost per kg alone — the cheapest rate often generates the highest total cost
- Treating high expedited freight as a logistics problem — it is almost always an upstream planning or supplier delivery failure
- Reviewing carrier performance only at contract renewal — annual reviews miss months of accumulated cost drift
