# Job Descriptions & Interview Preparation: Summary

## Key Concepts

- **Four JD Principles**: Candidate perspective first ("you will"), lead with the work, calibrate requirements ruthlessly (Essential vs Beneficial, max 5 essential), inclusive language by default
- **Essential vs Beneficial Test**: "Could a highly capable candidate do this job without this?", yes means Beneficial, no means Essential. Most JDs misclassify 10+ Beneficial requirements as Essential
- **Inclusive Language Check**: Automatically applied by /jd, flags gender-coded (rockstar, ninja), age-coded (young and dynamic), experience-specificity (years of tool rather than proficiency), and culture-coded language
- **Competency Mapping**: Every JD essential requirement maps to an interview competency; every interview question maps back to a JD requirement, alignment is the quality check
- **Scoring Rubrics**: 1-4 scale with described observable behaviours at each level, recorded independently before debrief discussion

## Skills Practised

- `/jd` (custom hr-operations plugin): Writes candidate-first job descriptions with automatic inclusive language check and required salary range
- `/interview-prep` (official human-resources plugin): Generates structured interview kits with competency mapping, behavioural and situational questions, and scoring rubrics

## Key Takeaway

The most powerful thing /jd does is force the essential/beneficial calibration: the discipline of justifying each requirement using the test. Organisations that apply this test consistently find they have been deterring qualified candidates for years by listing beneficial requirements as essential.
