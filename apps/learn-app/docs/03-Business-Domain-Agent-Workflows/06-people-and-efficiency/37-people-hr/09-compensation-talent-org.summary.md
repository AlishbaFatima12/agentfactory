# Compensation, Talent & Org Planning — Summary

## Key Concepts

- **Internal mobility imperative**: Industry research suggests assessing internal candidates first is both more cost-effective and a retention signal — overlooked high-potential employees typically leave.
- **Compensation percentile bands**: 25th (below market) / 50th (median) / 75th (above market) / 90th (top of market). Used to benchmark a role and check internal equity before making a promotion offer.
- **Six-dimension talent assessment**: The `/match` framework evaluates Critical skills, Experience, Performance trajectory, Readiness indicators, Development areas, and Motivation. Each dimension has its own rating scale.
- **Development gap types**: EXPERIENCE GAP (has aptitude, needs opportunity), SKILL GAP (needs to build capability), MINDSET GAP (assumptions need to shift). Gap type determines the development intervention.
- **Readiness classification**: READY NOW / READY IN 6 MONTHS / READY IN 12 MONTHS / DEVELOPING / NOT A FIT. Determines action, not just assessment.
- **Succession conversation structure**: OPENING (acknowledge potential) → MIDDLE (describe pathway conditionally) → INVITE (ask about their career intent) → CLOSE (agree one concrete next step with follow-up date).
- **Span-of-control benchmarks**: 5–8 direct reports is healthy. IC-to-manager ratio of 6:1–10:1 is healthy. Violations signal structural risk.

## Skills Practised

- `/comp-analysis`: Market benchmarking at 25th/50th/75th/90th percentile bands; internal equity check for a promotion offer
- `/match`: Six-dimension internal talent assessment; readiness classification; development plan for READY IN N MONTHS candidates
- `/org-planning`: Structural impact modelling when an internal promotion changes reporting lines; span-of-control analysis

## Key Takeaway

The three skills in this lesson are sequential, not parallel: benchmark the comp so you know what you can offer, assess talent so you know who is ready and what they need, then model the org structure so you know what changes when they move. Talent decisions made without all three inputs are incomplete.
