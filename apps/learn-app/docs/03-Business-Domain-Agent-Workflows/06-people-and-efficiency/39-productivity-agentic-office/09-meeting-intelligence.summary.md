### Core Concept

Meeting intelligence operates across three phases — before (prep brief), during (D/A/F/Q/R notes), and after (structured synthesis) — and the preparation phase is the highest-leverage of the three because it changes what happens in the meeting, not just how it is recorded afterwards.

### Key Mental Models

- **Before as the Multiplier**: A prep brief that surfaces stakeholder dynamics, prior decisions, and what each agenda item actually needs (decision vs discussion) changes how the meeting unfolds. Without it, people attend; with it, people lead.
- **D/A/F/Q/R as a Decision Rule**: Coding notes during a meeting (Decision / Action / Fact / Question / Risk) works because it replaces organisational judgment with a classification decision — which one of five types is this? This frees attention for the discussion itself rather than deciding how to structure notes in real time.
- **Specificity as the Quality Standard**: Decisions must be understandable six months later without other context. Actions must have one named owner and a specific date — not "the team" and not "ASAP". Deferred items must have a trigger for revisit, not just "later".

### Critical Patterns

- Run the prep brief 30 minutes before any meeting where you are presenting or deciding; include per-agenda-item context, decisions needed, your position, and at least one stakeholder note per key attendee
- Take notes using D/A/F/Q/R codes during the meeting in any order — do not tidy in real time; let the synthesis organise them
- Generate synthesis within two hours; use `/agentic-office:meeting-intelligence` with `type: synthesis`
- Number decisions D-YYYY-NNN and actions A-YYYY-NNN; store both in `work.local.md` decision and action logs to create searchable institutional memory
- Always confirm `work.local.md` update proposals — the synthesis proposes, never applies automatically

### Common Mistakes

- "The team will follow up" — shared ownership is no ownership; every action needs one named person who is accountable even if they delegate
- Treating meeting notes as discussion summaries — synthesis captures decisions, actions, and deferred items only; discussion is process, not output
- Omitting the "last time this group met" section in the prep brief — open actions from prior meetings are the most common single source of meeting failure
