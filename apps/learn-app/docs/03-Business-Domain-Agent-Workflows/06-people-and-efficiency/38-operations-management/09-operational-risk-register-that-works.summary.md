# Lesson 9 Summary: Operational Risk Register That Works

## What This Lesson Covers

Most risk registers fail not because organisations ignore risk, but because they conflate the existence of controls with the effectiveness of controls, produce residual scores that are aspirational rather than actual, and review the register annually rather than when circumstances change. This lesson teaches you to build a risk register that functions as a live management tool — using the `risk-assessment` auto-skill in Cowork.

## The Five Failure Modes

Risk registers fail when they are static (never updated), carry non-credible residual scores (inherent 15, residual 3, control: "verbal knowledge"), lack an escalation path, omit uncomfortable risks (key-person dependency, regulatory gaps), or contain vague mitigation plans with no owner, date, or target score.

## The 5x5 Scoring Methodology

Every risk carries two scores. Inherent risk is scored as if no controls exist — the raw exposure. Residual risk is scored after controls are applied. Score = Likelihood (1–5) × Impact (1–5). Bands: LOW (1–4), MEDIUM (5–9), HIGH (10–16), CRITICAL (17–25).

The critical rule: residual risk can only be lower than inherent when the controls are rated STRONG — reliably tested and evidenced. MODERATE controls justify a partial reduction. WEAK or ABSENT controls mean residual risk stays close to inherent.

## Control Effectiveness Ratings

STRONG (reliably tested, evidence available), MODERATE (partially addresses risk, some gaps, not fully tested), WEAK (exists on paper, unconfirmed effectiveness), ABSENT (no effective control — inherent equals residual).

## Using the risk-assessment Auto-Skill

The `risk-assessment` skill activates automatically from natural-language keywords: "risk", "risk register", "risk assessment", "mitigation". Students never type `/risk-assessment` — they describe the risk landscape they want assessed in natural language, including the organisation's risk appetite.

## Mitigation Plans

A mitigation plan is only useful if it specifies: the exact action, whether it reduces likelihood or impact (quantified), the target residual score, one named owner, and a target completion date. "Improve vendor management" is not a plan. "Pre-qualify a backup vendor for ERP by Q2; target likelihood 3→2, score 15→10, Owner: Operations Manager" is a plan.

## The Escalation Matrix

The escalation matrix converts the register from a document to a tool by defining exact score thresholds for when a risk escalates from Operations Manager to COO to Board. Thresholds must be score-based — not vague criteria like "if serious." Zero-tolerance scenarios (regulatory breach, data breach) always escalate to Board regardless of score.
