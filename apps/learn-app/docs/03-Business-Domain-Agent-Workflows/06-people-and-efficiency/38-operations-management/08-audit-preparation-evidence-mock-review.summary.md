# Lesson 8 Summary: Audit Preparation: Evidence and Mock Review

## What This Lesson Covers

Audit preparation is not about polishing documents; it is about confirming that the evidence that already exists is locatable, current, and can demonstrate what it claims to demonstrate. This lesson teaches structured audit preparation using the `/audit` command, building directly from the compliance obligation map produced in Lesson 7.

## The Core Insight

Two failures cause organisations to produce poor audit results. The first: evidence exists but cannot be located on audit day. The second: team members give incomplete or inconsistent answers because they were not briefed on scope and expectations. The `/audit` command addresses both by generating a week-by-week preparation plan with an evidence inventory and a staff briefing plan.

## Five Audit Types

Internal (own audit function), External (third-party), Regulatory (FCA, ICO, HMRC), Customer (client reviewing supplier), and Certification (ISO, PCI, SOC). Preparation depth should match stakes: regulatory audits have the highest stakes (enforcement action, fines, licence conditions), certification audits have moderate stakes (losing the certification), and customer audits have commercial stakes (contract suspension or renegotiation).

## From Compliance Map to Preparation Plan

The compliance obligation map from Lesson 7 is the input to `/audit`. Every obligation in the map becomes an audit area. The preparation plan converts each obligation from "status" to "evidence ready by date," with a week-by-week timeline, evidence inventory, gap closure priorities, and briefing plan.

## The Mock Audit

A mock audit simulates the auditor's questions before the real review. Its purpose is to find the gaps between knowing a control exists and being able to demonstrate it under questioning. Any gap identified in the mock audit that is not closed before the actual audit becomes a real finding: a mock audit that produces unfixed gaps leaves a paper trail demonstrating awareness.

## Audit Response Maturity

Audit responses are read as evidence of governance maturity. Defensive responses ("we disagree," "this has never caused a problem") damage the regulatory relationship. Mature responses acknowledge the finding without argument, identify the specific root cause, state corrective action already taken and systemic prevention planned, name one owner, and provide a target completion date. MAJOR findings require systemic corrective action , not just fixing the instance, but preventing recurrence.

## Connection to Lesson 7

This lesson begins where Lesson 7 ends: the compliance obligation map is the foundation for everything that follows. Organisations that build a compliance map (L07) and then prepare structured audit responses (L08) demonstrate an integrated compliance management approach: the kind that builds regulatory confidence over time.
