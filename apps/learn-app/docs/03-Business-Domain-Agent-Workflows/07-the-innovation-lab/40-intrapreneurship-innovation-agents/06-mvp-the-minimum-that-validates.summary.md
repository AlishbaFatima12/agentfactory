# Lesson 6 Summary: MVP — The Minimum That Validates

## What You Learned

You learned that the MVP (Minimum Viable Product) is not the minimum product you can ship — it is the minimum product that tests the most critical assumptions at the lowest possible cost. The lesson introduced the feature inclusion criterion (every feature must test a TIER 1 or TIER 2 assumption), the explicit exclusion list (every excluded feature needs a documented rationale for why it tests no critical assumption at this stage), and the success and failure criteria framework. You produced a complete MVP design document for the AP automation case: 5 features in, 6 features explicitly excluded, 4 success criteria mapped to specific assumptions, 3 failure criteria with pivot implications, and an 8-week build plan.

## Key Takeaways

- **The exclusion list is as important as the inclusion list.** What you choose NOT to build defines the MVP. An explicit exclusion list with documented rationales is what separates a scoped MVP from a scaled-down version 1.
- **Success criteria must be specific and measurable.** 'Users like it' and 'good adoption' are not success criteria. '3 customers pay $500/month before the build' is. Each criterion maps to a specific assumption from the Lesson 5 map.
- **Failure criteria should be written before the pilot, not after.** Pre-agreed failure thresholds prevent motivated reasoning — the tendency to reinterpret ambiguous results as success after six weeks of emotional investment in the customer relationships.
- **MVP bloat is scope creep with better branding.** Adding TIER 3 features before TIER 1 and TIER 2 assumptions are validated is MVP bloat. The guard: for every feature candidate, ask which critical assumption it tests.
- **The build plan ends at pilot onboarding, not at 'done'.** The MVP exists to generate data. The plan ends when measurement begins — not when the feature list is complete.
