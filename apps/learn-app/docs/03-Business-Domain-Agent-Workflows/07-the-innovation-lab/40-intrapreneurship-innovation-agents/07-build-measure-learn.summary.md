# Lesson 7 Summary: Build-Measure-Learn

## What You Learned

You learned how to close the Lean Startup loop by analysing pilot results against the assumption map and success criteria established in Lessons 5 and 6. The BML analysis produces three types of output: validated assumptions, invalidated assumptions, and unexpected learnings. You applied the evidence quality hierarchy — where VALIDATED requires payment or sustained usage, not stated intent or enthusiasm — to the AP automation pilot results. You learned the eight pivot types (Zoom-in, Zoom-out, Customer Segment, Customer Need, Platform, Business Architecture, Technology, Channel) and the four conditions in the Pivot Decision Checklist. The AP pilot produced a PERSEVERE recommendation with two specific additions driven by unexpected learnings: handwritten invoice accuracy as a V1 priority (not V2 as originally planned) and remittance email automation as a quick win.

## Key Takeaways

- **VALIDATED requires behaviour, not opinion.** Payment or unprompted usage is validated. 'They said they loved it' is anecdotal. The BML analysis applies this standard consistently to every assumption.
- **Unexpected learnings are often more valuable than expected ones.** The AP pilot's discovery that 30% of invoices were handwritten changed the product roadmap more than any expected finding. Unknown unknowns are revealed only by running the MVP with real customers.
- **INCONCLUSIVE is a valid result.** When the context is confounding (Pilot 3's high staff turnover), drawing a conclusion is premature. Redesign the test rather than treating INCONCLUSIVE as VALIDATED.
- **A good pivot preserves validated learning.** The pivot changes only the broken dimension — not everything. If the customer segment was right but the pricing was wrong, the pivot is on pricing alone.
- **Pivot-or-persevere is assumption-by-assumption, not overall.** A mixed pilot can produce a PERSEVERE on the business model while pivoting on the channel — or persevere everywhere with specific modifications driven by unexpected learnings.
