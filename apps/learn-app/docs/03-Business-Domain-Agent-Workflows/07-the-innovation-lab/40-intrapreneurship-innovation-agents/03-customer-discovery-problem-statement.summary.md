# Lesson 3 Summary: Customer Discovery and Problem Statement

## What You Learned

Customer discovery is the first and most important step in the DLA Stack — it converts assumptions about customer needs into evidence-based insight. You learned how to synthesise raw interview notes into a Jobs-to-be-Done map (functional, emotional, and related jobs), rank pain points by frequency times severity, craft non-obvious insight statements, and frame the problem using the "How Might We" format. The `/discovery` skill accelerates the synthesis pipeline without replacing the customer conversation itself.

## Key Takeaways

- **Discovery before ideation**: You cannot generate good solutions without understanding the problem. Every subsequent lesson in this chapter depends on what you learn from customers.
- **JTBD separates what from why**: Functional jobs reveal the workflow; emotional jobs reveal the buying decision. Products that solve functional jobs but ignore emotional jobs fail at adoption.
- **Pain ranking uses frequency times severity**: A rare catastrophic pain outranks a common annoyance. The ranking drives product direction.
- **Strong insights are non-obvious**: If you could have guessed the finding without conducting interviews, it is not an insight. Strong insights are specific, actionable, and evidence-based.
- **HMW frames the problem, not the solution**: A good HMW statement is open enough for multiple solution approaches but specific enough to focus your team.
