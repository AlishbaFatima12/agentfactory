# Lesson 12 Summary: Investor Pitch Deck

## What you learned

A great investor pitch deck is a narrative arc , not a product document. Each of the 9 slides (Hook → Problem → Solution → Market → Traction → Business Model → Team → Ask → Vision) has one job: produce a specific investor emotion that moves them toward conviction. The pitch is constructed from your existing venture data: the canvas, financial model, market sizing, and GTM strategy you built in Lessons 8–11.

## Key takeaways

- **Emotional engineering drives narrative structure.** The sequence of slides is not arbitrary. Problem precedes Solution so investors feel the pain before seeing the fix. The Ask comes second-to-last because conviction must precede term evaluation.
- **Pitch quality standards apply to every claim.** Every figure, adoption rate, and market number needs a source. Unsourced claims and banned phrases ("massive market opportunity", "conservative projections", "no direct competition") signal amateur thinking to experienced investors.
- **Traction hierarchy is your honesty check.** Revenue beats LOIs, which beat waitlists, which beat interviews. Know exactly where you sit and compensate with team strength and insight quality if traction is limited.
- **The 15 hardest questions reveal your weakest points.** You will avoid the most uncomfortable questions if you generate them yourself. Use the AI ; it will find them. The questions you are least prepared for are the ones that kill deals.
- **The executive summary is your cold-open.** 100 words that include problem, solution, traction, ask, and differentiation. No placeholder text. Works in isolation without the deck.
