# Lesson 11 Summary: Go-to-Market Strategy

## What You Learned

GTM strategy is how strategy meets execution. You learned to define an ICP with a buying trigger (the specific event that creates urgency , not just demographics), write a positioning statement using the For/Who/Our product/That/Unlike/We format, and design a channel strategy ranked by CAC efficiency across six archetypes. The lesson also covered a seven-step sales process, value-based pricing rules, and a 90-day GTM calendar with decision gates at Day 30 and Day 60.

## Key Takeaways

- **An ICP without a buying trigger is a description, not a strategy.** Customers without triggers browse but do not buy. Two identical demographic prospects have completely different closing probabilities depending on whether a trigger event has occurred.
- **Start with one channel.** Add a second only when the first is producing measurable results. Splitting focus across multiple channels simultaneously means none of them gets optimised.
- **Founder-led outreach teaches you the playbook.** Before handing off to SDRs, the founder must learn what message converts, what ICP characteristics predict adoption, and what the sales process feels like from the customer's side.
- **Paid trial over free trial.** Even at 50% of list price, a paid trial filters for customers with genuine urgency. Free trials attract browsers who churn before converting.
- **Decision gates force honest assessment.** The Day 30 gate asks: is the conversion rate above threshold? If not, change something before spending four more weeks on an approach that is not working.
