# Lesson 13 Summary: Innovation Sprints

## What you learned

Standard Agile sprints are designed for delivery — building known solutions iteratively. Innovation sprints adapt Agile for conditions where both the problem and the solution still have uncertainty. The defining adaptation is the dual goal: every innovation sprint must have a learning goal (which assumption is being tested?) alongside the delivery goal. Without the learning goal, you are running a product sprint and calling it innovation.

## Key takeaways

- **The learning goal is non-negotiable.** It references a specific assumption ID from your assumption map. If you cannot write a learning goal with an assumption ID, return to your assumption map (L05) before planning the sprint.
- **User stories link to assumptions.** Each story in an innovation sprint should answer: which assumption does this test? This transforms the backlog from a to-do list into a set of structured experiments.
- **Priority scoring makes tradeoffs explicit.** Assumption Risk Score (1–3) × Delivery Value Score (1–3) = Priority Score. The conversation about priority is more valuable than the number — it forces the team to agree on which assumptions are existential.
- **The retrospective's primary output is assumption updates.** Which assumptions changed status this sprint? What evidence supports the change? This is what keeps innov.local.md current. Team process discussion is secondary.
- **Switch formats at GROWTH stage.** When product-market fit is validated, switch to product sprints. Running innovation sprints at GROWTH stage adds overhead without reducing uncertainty — you are solving a different problem.
