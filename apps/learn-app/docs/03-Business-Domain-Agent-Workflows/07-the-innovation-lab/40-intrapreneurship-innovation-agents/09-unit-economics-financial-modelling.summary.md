# Lesson 9 Summary: Unit Economics and Financial Modelling

## What You Learned

Unit economics is the discipline of understanding whether one customer relationship is worth pursuing, before building the sales machine to acquire thousands of them. The core metrics are: sustainable CAC (not founder-led CAC, which is artificially low), LTV (and how churn determines it), the LTV:CAC ratio, payback period, and breakeven customer count. You also built a three-scenario revenue and runway model and ran a sensitivity analysis to identify the single most dangerous assumption.

## Key Takeaways

- **If you cannot make money on one customer, you cannot make money on a thousand.** Unit economics precedes projections, bad unit economics cannot be fixed by scale.
- **Churn is the most dangerous assumption.** A 4× difference in churn produces a 4× difference in LTV. If churn is assumed, flag it explicitly and validate it at Month 12.
- **Use sustainable CAC, not founder-led CAC.** Founder time at deferred salary is not what customer acquisition will cost when you hire a sales team. Plan with market-rate costs.
- **The conservative scenario is the one that matters most.** Investors stress-test the downside; your job is to show you can survive it.
- **The fundraising trigger must be 6 months before projected cash-out.** Fundraising takes time: a model that shows fundraising at Month 10 when cash runs out at Month 12 is a failure plan.
