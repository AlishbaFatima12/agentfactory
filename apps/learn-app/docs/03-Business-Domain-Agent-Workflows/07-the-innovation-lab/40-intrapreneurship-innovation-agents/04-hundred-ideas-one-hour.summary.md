# Lesson 4 Summary: Hundred Ideas, One Hour

## What You Learned

You learned why unstructured brainstorming underperforms and how the 100-idea sprint overcomes those limitations through structure. The sprint divides idea generation across 10 categories, Product, Business Model, Distribution, Analogy, Technology, Crazy/10x, Boring, Contrarian, Partnership, and Incumbent: each forcing a distinct creative lens on the same problem statement. You applied the DVF framework (Desirability, Viability, Feasibility) and the "Why Now?" test to filter 100 ideas to an actionable shortlist of 10, then selected one idea and ran a devil's advocate pressure test.

## Key Takeaways

- **The best ideas are in the second half.** The first 50 ideas are the ones the team was already carrying, original thinking begins after the obvious space is exhausted. The 100-idea target is not about volume for its own sake; it is about forcing yourself past the creative boundary.
- **DVF is a filter, not a formula.** Scores of GREEN/YELLOW/RED across Desirability, Viability, and Feasibility give you structured comparisons, but exceptional founder-specific advantages can outweigh a single YELLOW score.
- **The "Why Now?" test surfaces timing risk.** An idea that cannot answer "what changed in the last 2-3 years to make this possible or urgent?" may be a good idea with bad timing , or one that someone already tried and it did not work.
- **Pressure testing is assumption discovery.** Each objection the devil's advocate raises is a hidden assumption inside the idea. The pressure test converts your selected idea into the foundation for the assumption map in Lesson 5.
- **Category discipline matters as much as volume.** Without the 10-category structure, 100 ideas become 10 ideas repeated with slightly different wording. The Contrarian and Incumbent categories are especially valuable for non-obvious ideas.
