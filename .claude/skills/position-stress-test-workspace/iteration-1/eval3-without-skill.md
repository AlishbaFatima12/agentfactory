Good — this is exactly the kind of decision that needs adversarial pressure before it reaches a boardroom.

**Two ways to run this:**

- **Coach Mode** — You defend, I attack. You'll walk into that board meeting having already faced the hardest questions. This builds genuine preparedness.
- **Output Mode** — I produce the full stress test document for you. Faster, but you don't build the muscle.

Given you're presenting to a board, I'd recommend **Coach Mode** — board members will probe in real-time, and rehearsed answers from a document won't hold up the same way.

**Before we start the adversarial rounds, I need your Position Lock.** This crystallizes your thinking so we're stress-testing a real position, not a vague intent.

Give me:

1. **Position Statement** (1 sentence, specific) — not "I think we should consider..." but a committed stance.

2. **Three Strongest Arguments** — each with a claim AND evidence/reasoning. They should be distinct from each other.

3. **Stakeholder Cost Matrix** — every group affected, including the uncomfortable ones. Who benefits, who gets harmed, magnitude (Low/Medium/High).

4. **Confidence %** (0-100) — how confident are you this is the right call? Be honest, not performative.

5. **Reversal Trigger** — what specific, testable condition would make you change this recommendation? Not "if things go badly" — something concrete.

Take your time with this. The quality of the stress test depends on the quality of the Position Lock.
