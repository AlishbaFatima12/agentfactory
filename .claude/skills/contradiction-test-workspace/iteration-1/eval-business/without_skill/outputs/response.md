Both consultants are right about different things and wrong about different things. Let me break it down.

## Where each is strongest

**Consultant A is right that:**
- Compliance features are inherently enterprise-valued. SMBs generally don't buy compliance — they buy convenience.
- Unit economics matter. 20 accounts at $50k ($1M ARR) beats 500 accounts at $99/mo ($594k ARR) with far less support burden.
- A 12-person team cannot serve 2,000 SMBs well. Support load scales linearly with SMB count.

**Consultant B is right that:**
- 6-9 month sales cycles against 14 months of runway is genuinely dangerous. You might close 2-3 deals before cash runs out.
- Feedback velocity matters enormously pre-PMF. Enterprise customers give you quarterly feedback. SMBs give you weekly feedback.
- The "start SMB, move upmarket" playbook is real and proven.

## Where each is weakest

**Consultant A's blind spot:** Runway. They're giving you a strategy optimized for a company with 36+ months of cash and an existing sales team. You have neither. Veeva had Salesforce alumni and pharma connections from day one. Palantir had government contracts and Peter Thiel's network. These are not your conditions.

**Consultant B's blind spot:** "Compliance-lite" is a fantasy. Compliance is binary — you're compliant or you're not. A "lite" tier that doesn't actually make someone compliant is a feature nobody will pay for. The Slack/Zoom/Dropbox analogy also breaks: those are horizontal tools with obvious SMB value. Compliance software is not.

## The synthesis

Neither "enterprise-first" nor "SMB-first" — **mid-market first, with enterprise design.**

**Concretely:**

1. **Target companies of 200-1,000 employees.** They have real compliance needs (unlike SMBs), but their buying cycles are 2-3 months, not 6-9. They have budget authority that doesn't require board approval. Price at $15k-25k/year.

2. **Sell compliance as risk reduction, not as a feature.** Mid-market companies know they need compliance but can't afford a compliance team. Position as "your compliance department for $20k/year" — that's a fraction of one hire.

3. **Target 15-25 accounts in the first 8 months.** That's $300k-500k ARR with a team of 12 that isn't drowning. Each account gives you more signal than an SMB but closes faster than enterprise.

4. **Use those accounts as proof for enterprise expansion at month 12-14**, right as you'd be raising your next round. "We have 20 mid-market customers with 95% retention" is a fundable story.

5. **Don't build compliance-lite.** Don't dilute what makes you valuable. Instead, build compliance-full with simpler onboarding. The product stays the same; the implementation shrinks.

How many mid-market prospects can you get in front of in 30 days? If the answer is fewer than 10, Consultant B's runway concern becomes existential and you may need to take a different approach entirely — possibly a services-led wedge where you do compliance consulting using your own tool, converting consulting clients into SaaS clients. If you can get 20+ conversations going quickly, the mid-market path has room to work.
