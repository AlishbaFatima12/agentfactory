## Decision Document

**Decision Context:** Choose between a $5M strategic acquisition (team integrates into buyer) or a $3.5M PE buyout (team continues operating independently). 30-day decision window. Current runway is 8 months — not emergency territory, but not comfortable enough to walk away from both and fundraise from strength. Stakeholders: founding team, employees (split on preference), and existing investors. This decision is hard because the $1.5M premium comes with a real cost (loss of independence and team identity), while the lower offer preserves optionality but at a 30% discount and with continued execution risk.

**RECOMMENDATION:** Take the $5M strategic offer and negotiate a 12-month team retention package with role guarantees for key employees.

**REASONING (287 words):**

The $1.5M delta between offers is not the real decision — the real decision is whether your team's independent upside exceeds $1.5M on a risk-adjusted basis within the next 8-18 months. With 8 months of runway, taking the PE deal means you are immediately back in fundraising or revenue-pressure mode, except now with a PE firm's return expectations layered on top. Independence sounds good until you realize "independent but PE-backed with 8 months runway" is not actually independent — it is a different boss with a shorter leash.

The strategic buyer's $5M is a 43% premium. That is real money for your investors and your team. The morale split is important but manageable: the employees who want stability get exactly that, and the employees who want independence will leave eventually regardless — the question is whether they leave into a strong job market with retention bonuses or into a runway crisis 6 months from now.

What you give up is real: identity, control, the chance that your product becomes a $50M company. But that chance is discounted by the probability of actually getting there with 8 months of cash. If you had 24+ months of runway, the calculus changes dramatically — you could credibly say "we'll grow into a better exit." At 8 months, you are negotiating from a position that weakens every month.

The negotiation leverage is in the retention terms, not the headline number. Push hard for 12-month retention with defined roles, not just vesting cliffs. This is where you protect the team members who are anxious about integration. A strong retention package converts "getting acquired" into "getting hired with a bonus" for most of the team.

**CONFIDENCE:** 68%

**Why this level:** The financial logic strongly favors the strategic offer given the runway constraint — that pushes confidence up. But two significant unknowns pull it back down: I don't know the strategic buyer's integration track record (some acquirers destroy teams), and I don't know whether your revenue trajectory could credibly support a raise that resets the board entirely. If either of those breaks the wrong way, this recommendation is wrong. 68% reflects "clear lean with real uncertainty," not a coin flip and not a slam dunk.

**MISSING INFORMATION (ranked by decision impact):**

**#1: Strategic buyer's post-acquisition integration track record** — Impact: If their last 2-3 acquisitions resulted in >50% team attrition within 12 months, the "stability" argument collapses and the PE offer's independence becomes the better team outcome despite the lower price. This could reverse the decision entirely.

**#2: Current monthly revenue growth rate and pipeline** — Impact: If MRR is growing >15% month-over-month with a strong pipeline, the 8-month runway is misleading because you could credibly raise a Series A at better terms than either offer. This would change the decision from "which offer" to "reject both and raise."

**#3: PE firm's typical hold period and exit expectations** — Impact: If the PE firm expects a 3x return in 3 years, you are signing up for intense growth pressure with less capital than the strategic deal provides. If they are patient capital with a 7-year horizon, the independence has real value. This changes how attractive the $3.5M actually is in practice.

**REVERSAL TRIGGER:**

I would change my recommendation if: the strategic buyer's last two acquisitions show greater than 50% team departure within 12 months of close, OR if current MRR growth exceeds 15% month-over-month for the last 3 months with a qualified pipeline exceeding $500K ARR — because either condition fundamentally changes whether the $1.5M premium is worth the integration risk or whether both offers should be rejected outright.

---

**Next step:** The highest-leverage action right now is answering missing information item #1. Before your next conversation with the strategic buyer, ask for references from teams they've acquired in the last 3 years. If they hesitate or refuse, that is itself informative.
