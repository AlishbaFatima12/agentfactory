## DECISION DOCUMENT

**Decision Context:** Choose between two acquisition offers within 30 days. $5M strategic buyer (team integration) vs $3.5M PE firm (operational independence). 8 months runway remaining. Team sentiment is split between stability and autonomy.

---

**RECOMMENDATION:** Accept the $5M strategic buyer offer and negotiate a 6-month transition period with explicit role guarantees for key team members who want independence.

---

**REASONING (280 words):**

The $1.5M delta is significant — it's 43% more capital. With only 8 months of runway, the alternative to acquisition isn't "keep building indefinitely" — it's "raise again or die." That context reframes the independence question: the PE deal preserves operational autonomy, but it preserves it inside a company that was 8 months from running out of money. $3.5M buys time, but under a PE firm's return expectations, you'll face pressure to hit metrics that may be harder than reporting to a strategic parent.

The team morale split is real but manageable. People who want stability get exactly what they want. People who want independence are actually saying "I don't want to lose agency over my work" — which is a negotiable term in the strategic deal (role guarantees, reporting structure, retention packages), not an inherent feature of the PE deal. PE independence is conditional too — miss targets and the board will intervene.

What you're giving up: genuine operational autonomy, the ability to pivot without approval, and the identity of being an independent company. These matter. But they matter less than solvency, and the strategic buyer is paying a premium precisely because your team and technology have integration value. That premium disappears if you wait.

The risk: strategic acquisitions have a well-documented failure rate for talent retention. If the buyer's culture is toxic or integration is mismanaged, you lose the team AND the independence. This is why the transition terms matter more than the headline number.

---

**CONFIDENCE: 62%**

Why this level: The financial logic clearly favors the strategic deal, but acquisition outcomes depend heavily on cultural fit and integration execution — variables I can't assess from the outside. A meaningfully better PE term sheet or a toxic strategic buyer culture could flip this.

---

**MISSING INFORMATION (ranked by decision impact):**

**#1: Strategic buyer's integration plan and track record with prior acquisitions** — If their last 2-3 acquisitions saw >50% team attrition within 12 months, my recommendation reverses. Culture and integration execution are the single biggest risk to the $5M deal actually delivering $5M of value to your team.

**#2: PE firm's actual operating expectations (revenue targets, timeline to exit)** — If their return expectations require 3x growth in 2 years, the "independence" is illusory — you'd be under more pressure than with the strategic buyer. If expectations are modest, the PE deal becomes significantly more attractive.

**#3: Which specific team members fall on which side, and who among them is critical to the company's value** — If your 2-3 most valuable people strongly want independence and would leave under the strategic deal even with retention packages, the $5M headline number is hollow because the buyer is paying for the team.

---

**REVERSAL TRIGGER:**

I would change my recommendation if more than 2 of your top 5 most critical team members state they would leave within 6 months under the strategic acquisition, even with a market-rate retention package. At that point, the strategic buyer is acquiring a depleted team, and the PE deal's independence becomes the only path that preserves the asset the buyer is actually paying for.
