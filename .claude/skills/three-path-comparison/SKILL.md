# Three-Path Comparison

Finding the right human-AI balance is an operational skill, not a philosophical stance. Most people default to one extreme: either they do everything solo (wasting AI capability) or they delegate everything to AI (producing competent but generic output). The three-path comparison reveals, through direct experience, where each approach genuinely adds value.

This skill helps users analyze any problem through three lenses, then synthesize the comparison into actionable collaboration guidance.

## Two Modes

### Coach Mode (Experiential)

Guide the user through attempting all three paths themselves. This is the high-learning version -- the user builds judgment through direct experience.

1. Help them define the problem clearly
2. Have them attempt Path A (solo) first -- set a time constraint
3. Have them attempt Path B (pure AI) -- accept first AI response, no overrides
4. Have them attempt Path C (collaboration) -- genuine iteration with prompting, evaluating, modifying, re-prompting, and overriding
5. Guide the comparison analysis
6. Diagnose their collaboration style

**When to use Coach Mode:** The user wants to build their collaboration judgment. They say things like "I want to practice", "help me learn when to use AI", or "I want to try this exercise."

### Output Mode (Analytical)

Analyze the problem and recommend the optimal human-AI split without requiring the user to attempt all three paths. This is the fast version -- the user gets actionable guidance immediately.

1. Analyze the problem characteristics
2. Map each path's strengths and weaknesses for THIS specific problem
3. Recommend the collaboration approach with specific guidance on what to delegate, what to do solo, and where to iterate
4. Diagnose the likely collaboration pitfalls

**When to use Output Mode:** The user has a real task and needs to decide their approach. They say things like "should I use AI for this?", "what's the best approach for this project?", or "how should I split this work?"

## The Process

### Step 1: Define the Problem Clearly

Before comparing paths, ensure the problem is well-specified:
- What is the desired output? (document, strategy, code, analysis, creative work)
- What domain expertise does this require?
- What is the quality bar? (internal draft, client-facing, published)
- What are the constraints? (time, data access, domain knowledge)

### Step 2: Path A -- Solo Approach

Analyze what a human would produce working entirely alone, with no AI assistance.

**Strengths to identify:**
- Original thinking unconstrained by AI patterns
- Deep domain expertise applied without dilution
- Contextual awareness (organizational politics, culture, history) that AI lacks
- Emotional intelligence and judgment calls
- Novel framing that AI training data would not contain

**Weaknesses to identify:**
- Knowledge gaps the human cannot fill alone
- Time-intensive research and synthesis
- Blind spots from single-perspective thinking
- Formatting and structural inconsistencies
- Scope limitations from individual capacity

### Step 3: Path B -- Pure AI Approach

Analyze what AI would produce if given the problem with no human editing, overriding, or iteration. Accept the first response as-is.

**Strengths to identify:**
- Breadth of coverage (AI rarely misses obvious categories)
- Structural consistency and professional formatting
- Speed of first draft
- Pattern application from training data
- Baseline competence across many domains

**Weaknesses to identify:**
- Generic advice that could apply to any similar problem
- Missing context-specific nuance
- Hallucinated specifics presented with false confidence
- "Obvious best practices" without strategic prioritization
- Lack of genuine insight -- competent but not insightful
- Inability to make judgment calls that require values or priorities

### Step 4: Path C -- Collaboration Approach

Analyze what happens when human and AI genuinely collaborate: prompt, evaluate, modify, re-prompt, override where needed, iterate.

**Key collaboration moves to identify:**
- Where the human PROMPTED well (asked the right question)
- Where the human EVALUATED accurately (caught AI weakness)
- Where the human MODIFIED effectively (improved AI output)
- Where the human RE-PROMPTED strategically (pushed for depth)
- Where the human OVERRODE correctly (replaced generic with specific)
- Where the human should have ACCEPTED (AI was right, human instinct was wrong)

### Step 5: Comparison Analysis

This is the most important step. Compare all three paths to answer:

1. **Where was solo stronger?** Identify specific elements where human-only thinking produced something AI could not replicate -- original insights, contextual judgment, values-based decisions.

2. **Where did pure AI fail?** Identify specific weaknesses in the unedited AI output -- generic recommendations, missing context, false confidence, hallucinated details.

3. **Where did collaboration outperform both?** Identify the specific value-adds that only happened through iteration -- where the combination exceeded what either could do alone.

4. **What SPECIFIC value did human judgment add?** Not vague claims ("I made it better") but concrete identification of what the human contributed that AI could not.

5. **Where did human intervention make things WORSE?** Be honest -- sometimes the human's edits introduced errors, removed useful AI suggestions, or injected bias. Identify these moments.

### Step 6: Collaboration Style Diagnosis

Based on the analysis, diagnose the user's collaboration style:

**Too Deferential:** Accepts most AI output with minor cosmetic edits. The "collaboration" version is 90%+ AI. The human adds formatting but not judgment. Warning signs: "I just cleaned it up a bit", minimal overrides, accepting generic recommendations without questioning.

**Too Overriding:** Rejects most AI output and rewrites heavily. The "collaboration" version is 90%+ human with AI used only as a starting point to react against. Warning signs: "AI never gets it right", extensive rewrites, using AI only to generate something to disagree with.

**Well-Balanced:** Genuine back-and-forth where both human and AI contributions are visible in the final output. The human prompts strategically, evaluates critically, accepts what works, overrides what does not, and re-prompts for depth. Warning signs of balance: specific evidence of AI suggestions kept, specific evidence of human overrides, iteration visible in the process.

### Comparison Narrative (300-400 words)

Write a narrative answering: Where was solo stronger? Where did pure AI fail? Where did collaboration outperform both? What specific value did human judgment add? Where did human intervention make things worse?

The narrative must be HONEST. Do not default to "collaboration always wins." Sometimes solo is better (for deeply creative or values-laden work). Sometimes pure AI is sufficient (for well-structured, low-stakes tasks). The analysis should reflect the actual characteristics of THIS problem.

### Collaboration Style Diagnosis

Provide a clear diagnosis (too deferential / too overriding / well-balanced) with:
- Evidence supporting the diagnosis
- Specific recommendations for adjustment
- What to try differently next time

## Quality Checks

Apply these checks to every three-path comparison output:

1. **Honesty Check:** Does the analysis honestly identify where AI was BETTER than the human? Most people unconsciously bias toward human superiority. Flag this if the analysis never credits AI.

2. **Specificity Check:** Does the analysis identify SPECIFIC human value-adds, not vague claims? "I added my experience" is vague. "I replaced the generic market-sizing approach with our internal customer cohort data, which changed the growth projection from 15% to 8%" is specific.

3. **Collaboration Style Diagnosis:** Does the output include a clear diagnosis of collaboration style with evidence?

4. **Worse-Case Identification:** Does the analysis identify at least one place where human intervention made things WORSE? If not, the analysis is likely biased.

5. **Path B Fairness:** Was the pure AI path given a fair prompt? If the prompt was deliberately vague or bad, the comparison is rigged. The AI should receive the same problem statement as the human.

6. **Context Sensitivity:** Does the recommendation account for the specific characteristics of THIS problem, or is it generic advice about AI collaboration?

7. **Actionable Guidance:** Does the output end with specific, actionable recommendations for how to approach similar problems in the future?

## When Evaluating Existing Workflows

If the user asks you to evaluate their current AI collaboration approach (rather than comparing three paths for a new problem):

1. **Ask for evidence:** Request examples of their solo work, their AI prompts, and their final collaborative output.
2. **Look for the collaboration gap:** The difference between "using AI" and "collaborating with AI" is iteration. If they prompt once and edit the output, that is delegation with editing, not collaboration.
3. **Identify their default mode:** Most people have a default (deferential, overriding, or balanced) that they apply regardless of task type. Help them see the pattern.
4. **Recommend task-specific calibration:** Different tasks warrant different collaboration intensities. A status report might warrant full AI delegation. A strategic decision might warrant solo thinking with AI as a sounding board. Help them build a mental model for calibration.

## Thinking Scorecard

Rate these dimensions when evaluating a three-path comparison:

| Dimension | What It Measures | Weak (1-3) | Strong (7-10) |
|-----------|-----------------|------------|----------------|
| Path Analysis Depth | How thoroughly each path was explored | Surface-level pros/cons list | Specific examples of what each path produces, with evidence |
| Comparison Honesty | Willingness to credit AI and criticize human contributions | "Human always adds value" bias | Identifies cases where AI was better AND where human made things worse |
| Value-Add Identification | Specificity of human contribution claims | "I improved it" / "I added my expertise" | "I replaced X with Y because Z, which changed the outcome from A to B" |
| Collaboration Style Awareness | Accuracy of self-diagnosis | Cannot identify own pattern | Clear evidence-based diagnosis with specific adjustment recommendations |
| Recommendation Actionability | Whether guidance is usable for future decisions | "Use AI for some things and not others" | "For tasks with characteristic X, delegate Y to AI and do Z yourself because..." |
