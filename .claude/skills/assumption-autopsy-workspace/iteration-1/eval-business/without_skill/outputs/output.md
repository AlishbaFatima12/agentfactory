# Assumptions You Might Be Missing: SaaS for Law Firms

Here are some assumptions you might not have considered:

## What You Listed
- Law firms want cloud-based tools
- $99 is affordable
- LinkedIn ads will work

## Additional Assumptions to Consider

1. **Data Security Concerns**: Law firms deal with sensitive client data. You're assuming they'll trust a new cloud provider with privileged information. Many firms have strict data handling policies.

2. **Competitive Landscape**: There are already established players like Clio, PracticePanther, and MyCase. You're assuming your product offers something different enough to compete.

3. **Price Point**: While $99 seems affordable, some solo practitioners might find it expensive, while larger firms might see it as too cheap (and therefore not enterprise-grade). The price sends a signal about quality.

4. **Sales Cycle**: Law firms typically have longer decision-making processes. You're assuming you can get them to sign up quickly through ads, but legal software purchases often involve demos and partner meetings.

5. **Market Size**: "Small law firms" is broad. Solo practitioners have very different needs than 15-person firms.

6. **Technical Adoption**: You're assuming lawyers are technically savvy enough to adopt new tools easily. Many smaller firms still rely on paper and legacy systems.

7. **Compliance**: Different states have different bar association rules about cloud storage of client files. You need to make sure you're compliant everywhere you operate.

8. **Churn**: Subscription businesses live and die by churn. If law firms sign up and leave after 3 months because onboarding was too hard, your unit economics won't work.

## Recommendations

- Talk to 20-30 small law firm owners before building
- Research Clio's pricing and feature set thoroughly
- Check bar association regulations in your target states
- Consider whether a freemium model might work better for initial adoption
- Think about integration requirements (court filing systems, accounting software)

These are the biggest blind spots I see. The pricing model and sales cycle mismatch is probably the most critical risk.
